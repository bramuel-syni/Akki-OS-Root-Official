[L4] The Mkondo Charter

[L5] The orchestration charter: the record matrix, the task-identity register, and the handoff contracts — one place where who writes, who reads, and who is acting is never ambiguous

[L6] PART I

[L8] What This Charter Is

[L9] [Heading 1] 1  Purpose

[L10] Two authorities now stand on one substrate: the governing authority of Documents 2 through 4, and the Watch of Document 8. Both mint records; both read records; both staff work from shared capacity. Every ambiguity that arises between them is one of three questions — who writes this record, who may read it, and under whose identity is this task acting — and scattering the answers across five documents recreates the ambiguity this charter exists to remove. Mkondo is the orchestration service that maintains three registers and enforces them; this charter is those registers, stated once, cited by both authorities as the same document.

[L11] Mkondo governs structure and holds no opinion about content. It admits nothing, judges nothing, detects nothing, and responds to nothing. Both authorities stand on it equally; the witness watches both across it. A question this charter answers is answered here — never by recollection, and never differently in two places.

[L12] [Heading 1] 2  The One-Record Law, Stated Precisely

[L13] Document 1 §4.3 holds: there is exactly one record, and nothing maintains a second. What the law forbids is a second, competing account of the same events. What it requires is exactly this matrix: one authoritative account per record class, with a single writing authority and named readers. The Watch minting the first account of its own events is the law working; two accounts of anyone's events would be the law broken. X and Y read the same record; X and Z mint new classes; nobody mints a rival.

[L36] [Heading 1] 4  Reading Discipline

[L37] Reads are as governed as writes. The Watch reads governance evidence to protect the estate and for no other purpose; governance reads Watch records for assurance and never to re-litigate a verdict; and neither authority's surfaces render the other's interior — the compliance owner sees that the Watch acted and why at the record level, never its thresholds or placements; the watch officer sees the run record's facts, never governance's deliberations. Same facts, two readings, by construction.

[L38] PART III

[L40] The Task-Identity Register

[L41] [Heading 1] 5  One Task, One Identity

[L42] Every task class in the platform maps to exactly one authority's identity and scope before it executes. An unmapped task class does not run — not runs-with-a-warning; does not run. Workers serve both authorities across time and never within a task; evidence crosses between authorities only through the matrix above, provenance-marked with the identity that produced it, never sideways through a dual-hatted task. The register's enforcement is structural: no credential scope exists that spans both authorities, so defect D8 — Document 7 §47 — is unrepresentable rather than policed.

[L59] Every crossing between services or authorities is a registered contract: what crosses, in what shape, under whose identity, with what receipt. An unregistered handoff is a finding. The contracts in force:

[L71] [Heading 1] 7  Change and Precedence

[L72] This charter changes by the same ceremony as any consequential rule — proposed, counter-signed, waited, applied, re-verified — with matrix changes carrying the slowest countdown class, because the matrix is the boundary of both authorities at once. Where this charter and any other document appear to disagree about who writes, who reads, or who is acting, this charter governs; where it is silent, the owning authority's canon governs; and a silence that mattered is a finding to close by amendment, never a gap to fill by recollection.

[L73] [Heading 1] 8  What This Charter Comes Down To

[L74] One account per record class, one identity per task, one contract per crossing — held in one short document both authorities cite. Seamlessness comes from the reads; boundaries come from the writes; and the whole point of writing it down is that nobody, human or machine, ever answers a matrix question from memory.
