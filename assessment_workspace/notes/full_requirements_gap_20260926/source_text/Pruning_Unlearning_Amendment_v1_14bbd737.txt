[B2] Pruning & Unlearning Amendment

[B3] 2 amendments · 1 new promise · 1 ruling · 9 September 2026

[B4R1] Applies to | dap_backend_spec_v1 (new DAP-37, DAP-38) · Security Assurance Promise Set (S-26)

[B4R2] What this closes | Two questions raised while defining artefact egress that DAP-31/DAP-36 did not answer: can a fine-tuned model be made smaller and cleaner before it leaves, and what happens to an artefact already registered or egressed when the right to a unit it trained on is later withdrawn.

[B4R3] Honesty condition stated up front | Pruning reduces memorisation empirically; it does not prove its absence. DP-SGD (DAP-36) is the guarantee; pruning is a second reduction on top of it. This amendment does not present pruning as a substitute for differential privacy, and DAP-37 says so explicitly.

[B5] T-3   Artefact Pruning Before Egress

[B6] TARGET  dap_backend_spec_v1 — new DAP-37, extending DAP-31 and DAP-36.

[B7] CURRENT  DAP-31 gates egress on a reproduction probe. DAP-36 trains with differential privacy by default. Neither addresses reducing an artefact's size or memorisation surface between acceptance and egress — an artefact either passes DAP-31 as trained, or it doesn't.

[B8] AMENDED — normative text

[B9] DAP-37  A fine-tuned artefact MAY be pruned — magnitude pruning or an equivalent structural reduction — between MOAC acceptance and DAP-31's distance gate, as a ruled optional step. Pruning is a second, empirical reduction in memorisation surface on top of DAP-36's differentially private training; it is never a substitute for it, and this document does not present it as one. A pruned artefact MUST re-pass MOAC's acceptance criteria — pruning can degrade performance below the accepted bar, and acceptance is re-evaluated, not assumed to survive the change. A pruned artefact's hash differs from its pre-pruning hash; it is a new artefact for every purpose DAP-10 and DAP-31 govern, including a fresh pass through the distance gate at the ruled threshold. Pruning ratio and method are ruled values, ratified alongside DAP-31's own probe parameters.

[B10] MECHANISM  Magnitude pruning removes the weights contributing least to model output, which empirically also reduces the model's capacity to have memorised specific training examples — a smaller, sparser artefact has less room to encode any one unit's content precisely. This is a capacity reduction, not a guarantee; DAP-31's probe, re-run on the pruned artefact, is what actually confirms the result.

[B11] DERIVATION  Han, Mao, and Dally, “Deep Compression” (2015) — magnitude pruning and its empirical effect on memorisation capacity. DAP-31 (the gate this feeds back through). DAP-36 (the guarantee this does not replace).

[B12] PRINCIPLE  A smaller artefact is not a safer artefact by assertion. It is a candidate for re-testing, and DAP-31 is what turns the candidacy into a fact.

[B13] T-4   Unlearning on Rights Withdrawal

[B14] TARGET  dap_backend_spec_v1 — new DAP-38. Rules Service (registry query, RBM-20) as the mechanism that makes affected artefacts findable.

[B15] CURRENT  No requirement addresses what happens when a contributor withdraws consent, or a unit's licence expires, after an artefact trained on that unit has already been registered or has already egressed under DAP-10. DAP-31 gates what leaves; nothing governs what happens to what already left.

[B16] AMENDED — normative text

[B17] DAP-38  When a unit's rights status changes to withdrawn — consent revoked, licence expired, or an equivalent event — every artefact whose training manifest cites that unit is identified via the registry (a query, per RBM-20's design, not a search) and is either re-derived without the unit or recalled. Re-derivation trains a replacement artefact excluding the withdrawn unit, using the same run configuration recorded in the original training manifest, and passes it through DAP-31 and MOAC as a new artefact before it replaces the original in the registry. Recall withdraws the original artefact's registry entry and, where the artefact was already delivered under DAP-10, notifies the receiving party that the artefact's rights basis has changed. A unit's contribution to the estate release budget (DAP-35) is NOT refunded on unlearning — the privacy loss from the original release already occurred; unlearning restores the artefact's cleanliness going forward, not the budget already spent.

[B18] MECHANISM  Sharded, isolated training (SISA — Sharded, Isolated, Sliced, Aggregated) makes re-derivation tractable by limiting retraining to the shard containing the withdrawn unit rather than the whole model; DAP-36's DP-SGD defaults make the retrained shard's own privacy accounting straightforward, since the accountant already runs per training job. Where SISA-style sharding was not used for the original run, re-derivation means a full retrain, and §5 R-12 rules whether that is mandatory or whether recall alone suffices in that case.

[B19] DERIVATION  Bourtoule et al., “Machine Unlearning” — SISA (2021). Guo et al., “Certified Data Removal from Machine Learning Models” (2020) — the formal standard re-derivation approximates. Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 s.40 (erasure). GDPR Art. 17 (right to erasure). CA1-N3, already ruled: raw source and five-ring units get real erasure; a trained model is flagged via A17.15's rights-lapse mechanism rather than untrained on demand — DAP-38 is that same discipline's extension to artefacts DAP now governs.

[B20] PRINCIPLE  The erasure right does not stop at the database. A model that carries a unit's influence is still carrying it, whatever the registry says about the source record.

[B21] This generalises CA1-N3's model-flagging answer, already ruled for the platform's own trained models, to every artefact DAP-9/DAP-10 admits an external party's model into or egresses — not a new principle, an extension of one already decided.

[B22] 3   New Promise

[B23R1] ID | Promise | Rests on

[B23R2] S-26 | The system will remove a unit's influence from every artefact that carried it when the right to that unit ends — by re-derivation or recall, findable by registry query, with the release budget's prior consumption left unrefunded. | T-3, T-4 (DAP-37, DAP-38). CA1-N3. SISA; certified removal; DPA s.40; GDPR Art. 17.

[B24] 4   Ruling

[B25] R-12  When the original training run was not sharded for isolated retraining, is re-derivation mandatory regardless of cost, or does recall alone satisfy DAP-38 where re-derivation is uneconomic? Neither this document nor CA1-N3 answers this; it is a genuine owner decision, not a technical one.

[B26] Nothing above is blocked by R-12. Where sharding was used, re-derivation is the norm and inexpensive enough that the question doesn't arise. R-12 matters only for the harder case, and DAP-38's recall path is fully specified either way — it is available now, pending only the ruling on when it's sufficient versus when re-derivation is required.
