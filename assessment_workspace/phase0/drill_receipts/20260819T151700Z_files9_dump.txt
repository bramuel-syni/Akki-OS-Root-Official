PART A — PROMISE #1: NO LEAKAGE OF CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION
A1 · The promise, in plain language
Confidential information does not leave the organisation's control. When the platform uses an external AI model to answer a question, nothing that identifies a person, or that the organisation has declared confidential, reaches that external model in a form that could expose it. The organisation can state, and prove afterwards, that this held for every single call — not most calls, not calls that passed review, every call.
A2 · The service that powers it
SyniSense, the boundary governor. SyniSense governs one thing and only one thing: whether data may cross an access point, and in what transformed state it crosses. It is the single point through which every outbound model call passes. It is not a monitor that watches calls and raises alarms; it is the mechanism through which the call is physically made, such that a call not transiting SyniSense cannot reach a model at all.
A3 · Definition of the promise
"No leakage" is defined precisely, because a vague definition cannot be engineered against:
Identifiers are removed before crossing. Any value by which a specific person could be recognised — a name, a national ID number, a phone number, an account number, a biometric template, or any combination that resolves to an individual — is replaced with a placeholder token before the payload reaches the external model, and restored only after the model's answer returns inside the perimeter.
Declared-confidential material is removed before crossing. Beyond legal identifiers, the organisation may declare categories or specific terms as confidential (Promise #2, Document 2). Anything matching those declarations is treated with the same removal-and-restoration discipline.
The removal is enforced structurally, not by policy. It is not possible to make an outbound model call that skips the removal step, because there is no code path to the model that does not pass through the removal step. This is the difference between a rule people are asked to follow and a rule the system cannot violate.
Every crossing is provable after the fact. For each call, the organisation holds evidence that the call occurred, that removal was applied, and under what custody — without that evidence itself exposing the confidential material or the credential used.
A4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the organisation experiences (human-facing):
A person or agent inside the organisation asks a question of the platform in the ordinary way. They are not asked to redact anything themselves, not asked to approve a security posture per call, and not asked to route their question specially. The protection is invisible to honest use — the question is asked, the answer comes back complete and re-identified, and the person need never know a placeholder ever stood in for a real name.
Where a request cannot be safely served — because what it asks for cannot be answered without exposing something that may not cross — the person receives a refusal that states plainly why, and what they can do instead. A refusal is a defined, first-class outcome, not an error or a timeout. (The refusal vocabulary is closed and shared platform-wide; see Document 16 §8.)
What a consuming system experiences (machine-facing):
A service calling the platform's answering interface receives either a complete answer with its identifiers restored, or one of the defined refusal shapes — never a partial answer in which some identifiers were restored and others silently were not.
The external model, on the far side of the crossing, receives a payload in which confidential values are placeholder tokens. It never receives the mapping between token and real value; that mapping does not leave the perimeter and does not outlive the individual call.
What is produced and retained:
Per crossing, a trust receipt: evidence that the call happened, under what custody, without exposing the credential itself. What the receipt must contain is specified in A5.7 below.
A5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
A5.1 · The single chokepoint [CANON] There is exactly one function in the entire codebase that invokes the external model client. Every outbound model call — from any engine, any application, any task — is made through that one function. This is not a convention that developers are asked to honour; it is enforced by an automated negative-scan test that fails the build if any module other than the chokepoint function imports or invokes the model client. The guarantee is placed at the narrowest point in the system rather than the most convenient, because custody enforced at an orchestrator is custody a new call path can miss, whereas custody enforced at the sole invocation cannot be missed — there is no other invocation through which to miss it.
A5.2 · The custody sequence [CANON] Every model invocation transits three steps in fixed order, and no path is authorised to skip any of them:
De-identify. The inbound payload passes through the de-identifier, which replaces identifiers and declared-confidential values with placeholder tokens and records the token-to-value mapping in a per-request custody envelope.
Invoke. The de-identified payload is sent to the external model through the chokepoint function.
Re-identify. The model's returned answer passes back through the mapping, which restores the real values in place of the placeholder tokens, producing the complete answer for the internal consumer.
A5.3 · The per-request envelope does not outlive the call [CANON] The token-to-value mapping is held in a custody envelope scoped to that single request. It is created when the request enters the crossing and destroyed when the answer is returned. It is never persisted to disk as a standing record, never shared between requests, and never available to any consumer after the call completes. This is a load-bearing invariant: the mapping is the one thing that, if retained, would reconstitute exactly what the crossing exists to protect.
Interaction with Promise #4 (Document 2): Promise #4 requires that two files — the question as asked, and the extracted data before redaction — be retained and passed to the model as a two-item context. Those retained files are governed by a different custody class than this per-request mapping envelope, and the distinction is deliberate and must not be collapsed: the mapping envelope dies with the call; the Promise #4 files are retained under their own stated terms. Document 2 specifies those terms in full. Neither document may weaken the other's invariant; Document 16 §3 holds the reconciliation as a shared-custody ruling.
A5.4 · Tenant-scoped entity resolution [CANON] De-identification is not limited to what a general-purpose identifier recogniser happens to detect. Each tenant supplies the set of entities that must be recognised for their instance — the specific people, accounts, and terms that matter in their estate — so that de-identification catches organisation-specific identifiers a generic recogniser would miss. This tenant entity set is the same mechanism Promise #2 (Document 2) allows the DPO to extend with declared-confidential terms; it is named here and specified for authoring in Document 2.
A5.5 · The outbound network is closed except at the crossing [CANON] Exactly one path leaves the organisation's perimeter, and it is the custody boundary. No other module in the platform holds outbound network capability. This is enforced two ways at once:
a network-layer allowlist that refuses outbound connections originating from anywhere except the crossing, and
a static-analysis build gate that fails the build if any module other than the crossing acquires outbound network capability. Compute does not leave the perimeter either: models the organisation hosts, and GPU capacity the platform allocates, run inside the perimeter. Only where an external frontier model is genuinely required does the custody boundary govern that single crossing. (The perimeter-bound compute guarantee is stated in full for Promise #10, Document 11; it is named here because it is part of why "no leakage" holds — there is nowhere else for data to go.)
A5.6 · Structural impossibility, not policed prohibition [CANON] The platform's enforcement doctrine is that a prohibition enforced by review is eventually violated, whereas a prohibition enforced by a function signature, a type, or a schema cannot be violated without the violation failing to compile or failing to persist. This promise is built to that doctrine: the chokepoint's sole-invocation property, the fixed custody sequence, and the closed outbound network are all structural, not procedural. (This doctrine is platform-wide and is stated once in Document 16 §2; it is applied here.)
A5.7 · The trust receipt [CANON] A trust receipt is emitted per invocation. It is the key-custody envelope: evidence that the call happened and under what custody, constructed so that:
the credential used to reach the model is never logged or included; its presence is surfaced only as a boolean probe at module load (present → live path; absent → sandbox fixture path), and nothing downstream branches on the credential's actual value;
the receipt records that de-identification was applied and re-identification completed;
the receipt is signed and bound to the organisation alone, such that it cannot be reproduced or forged elsewhere;
the receipt is written to the one authoritative record (there is exactly one record; Document 16 §5 holds the record-ownership matrix).
A5.8 · Two custody pathways, both receipted, neither bypassing the other [CANON] The platform has two custody pathways: the synthesis path through the chokepoint (text and structured payloads to a language model), and the perception path through the perception router (for example, audio reaching a model that reads it). Both emit receipts. Neither pathway is a way around the other. A promise of "no leakage" that held only for the text path while audio crossed ungoverned would be false; both paths carry the same custody obligation. (The perception path's ingestion is specified for Promise #15, Document 9; its custody obligation is stated here because it is part of this promise.)
A5.9 · Grounding failure is a refusal, not a degraded answer [CANON] Where the model returns something that cannot be grounded in permitted material, the correct behaviour is refusal, not a weaker answer offered anyway. This is stated in full for Promise #8 (Document 4); it is named here because it bounds what "a complete answer" in A4 means — a complete answer is one that is both re-identified and grounded, and the absence of either yields a defined refusal, never a silent partial.
A6 · What must be true for the service to deliver as intended
The negative-scan test exists and runs on every build. If the test that enforces sole-invocation is absent, disabled, or not run, the entire promise degrades from structural to procedural. The test is itself a load-bearing component, not a convenience.
The de-identifier's recogniser plus the tenant entity set together cover the identifiers that actually occur in the estate. A structurally perfect chokepoint still leaks if the de-identifier fails to recognise an identifier as one. This is why tenant-scoped entity resolution (A5.4) is required and not optional: the completeness of recognition is as load-bearing as the completeness of the crossing.
The per-request envelope is genuinely destroyed, verifiably. There must be no path — log, cache, trace, debug dump, error handler — through which the mapping survives the call. An error thrown mid-crossing must not spill the envelope into a stack trace or a retained error object.
The outbound allowlist and the build gate are both active. Either alone is insufficient: the build gate stops new outbound capability being written; the network allowlist stops outbound traffic from anything that somehow acquired it anyway. Defence is the pair, not either half.
The credential is never the thing that varies behaviour. Nothing downstream of the boolean load-time probe may read, log, or branch on the credential value — otherwise the credential's absence or presence becomes a side channel.
Both custody pathways are held to this standard simultaneously. A new model modality added later (video, for example) must acquire a governed pathway before it acquires the ability to make a call, or it becomes an ungoverned fourth path and the promise silently becomes false.
PART B — PROMISE #3: INTENT, MEANING & QUESTION OBJECTIVE EXTRACTED BEFORE REDACTION
B1 · The promise, in plain language
When a question is stripped of its confidential and identifying content before being sent to an external model, the purpose of the question is not stripped with it. The platform captures what the question is actually trying to achieve — its intent, its meaning, and the objective it is pursuing — before redaction removes the sensitive specifics, so that the redacted question sent outward still carries enough of its purpose to be answered usefully, and the platform retains a faithful record of what was being asked and why.
B2 · The service that powers it
SyniSense at the crossing, performing an intent-and-objective extraction step that runs before the de-identify step of the custody sequence. This step draws on the platform's reasoning capability (Solva, Document 3) for the semantic reading of the question, and on the objective-freezing discipline (Northena, Document 5) for how a captured objective is recorded immutably. Both are named here and specified in their own documents; the requirement that the extraction happen, and happen before redaction, is stated in full here.
B3 · Definition of the promise
Three things are extracted, and the distinction between them is not cosmetic:
Intent — what the asker is trying to do (decide, verify, compare, summarise, discover). Intent is the action shape of the request.
Meaning — what the question is actually about, semantically, independent of the specific sensitive tokens it happens to contain. Meaning is what survives when "has patient Jane Wanjiru's creatinine trend worsened" becomes "has this individual's creatinine trend worsened" — the clinical question is intact; the identity is gone.
Question objective — the specific, bounded thing that would count as the question being answered. The objective is what lets the platform later judge whether the returned answer actually addressed the ask, or drifted.
"Before redaction" is the load-bearing phrase. If intent were inferred after redaction, from the already-stripped payload, some of the purpose would be irrecoverable — the redaction would have destroyed context the intent depended on. Extracting first means the platform reads the question while it is still whole, captures its purpose faithfully, and only then removes the sensitive specifics.
B4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the organisation experiences (human-facing):
Nothing additional is asked of the person. They ask their whole question naturally; the extraction is automatic and internal. The visible benefit is that answers to sensitive questions come back on-target rather than generic — because the purpose survived the redaction — and that the organisation can later see, for any past call, a faithful statement of what was being asked and to what end.
What a consuming system experiences (machine-facing):
The external model receives a redacted payload that nonetheless carries the extracted intent and meaning, so it can answer the real question rather than a hollowed-out version of it.
Downstream governance (the objective-scope check, the grounding check) receives the captured question objective as the frozen thing the answer will be judged against.
What is produced and retained:
A captured objective artifact for the request — the intent, the meaning, and the bounded objective — recorded immutably for the life of the request and its audit trail. How this artifact is frozen and why it cannot be edited mid-flight is specified for Promise #16/#18 (Document 5); its creation at this point in the crossing is the requirement here.
B5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
B5.1 · Extraction precedes de-identification in the fixed sequence [RULED] The custody sequence of A5.2 is refined: for any request carrying a question (as opposed to a raw data payload), the ordered steps are extract-intent → de-identify → invoke → re-identify. The extraction step reads the whole, un-redacted question, produces the objective artifact, and only then hands the payload to the de-identifier. No path performs de-identification before extraction for a question-bearing request.
B5.2 · The extraction reads meaning, not just surface tokens [CANON-adjacent] The meaning extracted must be robust to the removal of the sensitive tokens — that is, it must capture the question at a level of abstraction that survives de-identification. This is why the step uses the platform's reasoning capability (Solva) rather than a pattern match: recognising that a question is "a renal-function trend enquiry about one individual" requires reading it, not scanning it.
B5.3 · The captured objective is frozen, not editable [CANON] Once captured, the objective artifact is immutable for the life of the request. A change in what is being asked is a new request with a new artifact, never an edit of a live one. This is the same freezing discipline Northena applies to every admitted objective (Document 5); it is required here so that the thing the answer is judged against cannot be quietly moved after the fact to match whatever came back.
B5.4 · Declared and demonstrated intent are both recorded [CANON] The extracted intent is recorded as declared intent — what the question presents itself as trying to do. The platform separately observes, across the handling of the request, whether the conduct matches that declaration (for example, a request that declares "summarise" but is structured to extract identifiers behaves differently than it declared). Declared intent and demonstrated intent are both fields of the record, and divergence between them is tracked rather than assumed absent. (This declared-vs-demonstrated reconciliation is a pattern shared with the Watch's behavioural monitoring, Promise #6/#7, Document 3; Document 16 §6 holds the question of whether the two share one mechanism. Here, the requirement is that this crossing records both.)
B5.5 · Extraction failure is handled explicitly [RULED] Where the platform cannot confidently extract a coherent objective from a question — the question is malformed, internally contradictory, or too vague to bound — the request does not proceed to the model on a guessed objective. Instead it yields a defined outcome: either a request for the asker to clarify (where a human is in the loop), or a defined refusal shape (where a system is calling). The platform does not fabricate an objective in order to have one.
[RULED] Refuse or ask, never proceed on a best-effort guess — a fabricated objective defeats the grounding check downstream that depends on the objective being real.
B6 · What must be true for the service to deliver as intended
The extraction genuinely runs before de-identification, always, for question-bearing requests. If any path de-identifies first, the promise is false on that path — the purpose was destroyed before it was captured.
The extracted meaning is abstract enough to survive redaction but specific enough to be answerable. Too abstract and the outward question is useless; too concrete and it smuggles the sensitive specifics back in. This is a genuine quality bar on the extraction, measurable on the organisation's own material.
The captured objective is the same object the downstream grounding and scope checks consume. If the objective captured at the crossing and the objective the answer is judged against are two different records, they can disagree, and the audit becomes a story rather than a proof. There must be one objective artifact, created here, consumed downstream.
The freeze is real. If the objective artifact can be edited after capture, then "what was being asked" can be rewritten to match what came back, and the record no longer proves anything.
Extraction failure has a defined, non-fabricating outcome. The system must never invent an objective to keep a request moving; the failure path must exist and be exercised.
RULINGS APPLIED TO THIS DOCUMENT
B5.5 [RULED] — On failed objective extraction: refuse/ask. Settled.
Cross-reference to Document 2 — The Promise #4 retained-file custody class is reconciled with the per-request envelope invariant (A5.3): both files retained in full, but re-governed through full masking at every subsequent read, never just at write. See Document 2 §B5.4 and Document 16 §3 for the ruling in full.
Cross-reference to Document 16 §6 — Ruled: the declared-vs-demonstrated divergence tracking at this crossing (B5.4) and the Watch's behavioural reconciliation remain two deliberately separate mechanisms — Solva and the Watch are different authorities, and no mechanism spans both. See Document 16 §6.

PART A — PROMISE #2: THE USER-EDITABLE CONFIDENTIALITY LIST
A1 · The promise, in plain language
The organisation decides what counts as confidential, and can change that decision without engineering help. Beyond the identifiers the platform already recognises by law, the Data Protection Officer can declare additional categories, terms, or specific values as confidential — a project codename, a client list, an unreleased financial figure, a proprietary formula — and once declared and approved, the platform treats those declarations with the same removal-and-restoration discipline it applies to legal identifiers at the crossing. The list is living: things can be added when they become sensitive and retired when they no longer are, through a governed process.
A2 · The service that powers it
Two services acting together:
SyniSense's tenant entity resolution — the mechanism that, at the crossing, recognises the specific entities and terms that must be de-identified for this organisation. The confidentiality list is the authored extension of this entity set. (The crossing-time behaviour is specified in Document 1 §A5.4; this document specifies how the set is authored and governed.)
The platform's rule-and-declaration machinery — the same governed authoring, counter-signature, and change-ceremony apparatus that governs every consequential change in the platform. The confidentiality list is not a special-purpose store; it is a managed governed value under that apparatus.
A3 · Definition of the promise
"User-editable" is defined precisely:
The editor is the DPO or the compliance function, not an engineer and not an end user. Authoring confidentiality is an act of governance, held by the office accountable for it.
Declarations may be expressed at three levels of specificity: - Exact values — a specific string, number, or identifier that must never cross (a named individual's account number; a specific codename). - Patterns — a described shape that matches a family of values (an internal project-reference format; an employee-ID pattern). This is the "regex" capability in the owner's framing: a rule that matches by form, not only by literal. - Categories — a named class of information the organisation treats as confidential (for example "unreleased pricing," "M&A working material"), which the platform then recognises through the reasoning layer, not only by literal or pattern match.
"Editable" means governed-editable, not freely mutable. Adding a declaration and removing a declaration are not symmetric operations, because they carry different risk — see A5.4. A living list does not mean an unguarded one.
A4 · Delivery experience expectations
Per the owner's ruling, the DPO authors confidentiality through a conversational workflow with objective-shaping capability — the DPO states what they want to protect in their own words, and the platform helps shape that statement into a precise, testable declaration, iterating until the declaration actually expresses the DPO's intent. The workflow exposes five governed operations, each a defined state transition on a confidentiality declaration:
Generate — the DPO describes what should be confidential; the platform produces a candidate declaration (exact value, pattern, or category) that captures it, together with a plain statement of what that declaration would and would not match, so the DPO can see its reach before adopting it.
Review — the platform shows the DPO the consequence of the candidate: what it would catch, and — where computable against the measured estate — examples of what it would newly redact, so the reach is concrete rather than abstract.
Edit / contribute — the DPO refines the candidate in dialogue (narrow it, broaden it, add an exception), and the platform reshapes the declaration accordingly. This is the objective-shaping loop: it continues until the declaration matches the DPO's actual intent.
Adopt — the DPO accepts the shaped declaration as the one they want. Adopt places the declaration into the pending state; it is not yet live.
Approve — the declaration is committed into force through the governed change ceremony (A5.4). Approval is the act that makes it binding at the crossing.
What the DPO produces: a precise, governed confidentiality declaration, recorded with who authored it, who approved it, when, and exactly what it matches — added to the living confidentiality list.
What a consuming system experiences: from the moment a declaration is in force, the de-identifier at the crossing recognises and removes matching material on every subsequent call, with no per-call action by anyone.
[RULED] no front-end in this workflow: the five operations above are specified as backend states and transitions. Their rendering — how the chat interface looks, how the buttons are laid out — is deliberately not specified here.
A5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
A5.1 · The list extends the tenant entity set consumed at the crossing [CANON] An approved declaration becomes part of the tenant-scoped entity set that SyniSense's de-identifier consults on every crossing (Document 1 §A5.4). There is exactly one such set per tenant, and the confidentiality list is its authored portion. A declaration that is adopted but not approved is not in that set and does not affect any crossing.
A5.2 · The objective-shaping loop produces a testable declaration [PROPOSED] The Generate/Edit dialogue must resolve to a declaration that is mechanically testable — an exact value, a pattern with defined match semantics, or a category bound to a reasoning-layer recogniser — never a free-text instruction that only a human could interpret. The point of the shaping loop is to convert the DPO's natural-language intent into something the de-identifier can actually enforce. A declaration that cannot be tested against a payload cannot be enforced and must not be adoptable.
A5.3 · Review shows reach against measured material only [CANON-adjacent] Where the Review step shows examples of what a candidate declaration would redact, those examples are drawn from the portion of the estate that has been measured, and anything not yet measured is stated as such rather than implied to be clean. The DPO is never shown a false picture of completeness. (This honesty-about-the-unmeasured discipline is platform-wide; Document 16 §2.)
A5.4 · Adding and removing are asymmetric, by governed ceremony [CANON]
Adding a declaration takes effect through approval and is, relative to removal, the safe direction — it protects more, not less.
Removing a declaration — that is, ceasing to treat something as confidential — is the consequential direction, because it exposes at the crossing what was previously withheld. Removal therefore requires counter-signature and runs the governed change ceremony: proposed → second signature → visible countdown during which it can be cancelled → applied → re-verified. This is the same ceremony that governs every consequential governance change platform-wide; it is not re-implemented for confidentiality. (The ceremony is specified in full in Document 14; it is named here because removal from the confidentiality list is one of its cases.)
A5.5 · The whole list is itself a governed record [CANON] Every declaration carries its authoring provenance — who generated it, who edited it, who approved it, when each occurred, and every prior version of the declaration. Nothing is deleted from this history; a retired declaration keeps its place in the record, marked retired. There is one authoritative record of the confidentiality list (Document 16 §5).
A6 · What must be true for the service to deliver as intended
The shaping loop reliably converts intent into an enforceable declaration. If the DPO believes they have protected "all unreleased pricing" but the adopted declaration only matches one format of it, the promise is silently false. The Review step's honest statement of reach (A5.2, A5.3) is what guards against this and is therefore load-bearing.
Approval, not adoption, is the gate to enforcement. If an adopted-but- unapproved declaration ever affected a crossing, the governance boundary between "the DPO is drafting" and "the organisation has decided" would be erased.
Removal genuinely runs the ceremony. If a declaration could be removed without counter-signature and countdown, a single person could quietly expose previously protected material, and the promise would depend on trust rather than structure.
The list and the crossing consume the same entity set. If the authored list and the set the de-identifier actually reads are two stores, they can diverge, and a declaration the DPO believes is live may not be. One set, one source.
PART B — PROMISE #4: THE DUAL-FILE RECORD
B1 · The promise, in plain language
When the platform sends a question to an external model, it does not send one merged blob. It sends two separate items: the question exactly as the user asked it, and the extracted data that question needs answered — as two distinct files. Inside the crossing these two files are treated as unrelated — each is assessed on its own, neither aware of the other — so that the assessment of one cannot be weakened or confused by the other. After the crossing's processing, the two are re-associated as one, and the external model receives them together as a two-item context for a single query.
B2 · The service that powers it
SyniSense at the crossing, extended with a two-file handling contract: a splitting-and-independent-assessment stage before the model call, and a re-association stage that assembles the two-item context for the call itself.
B3 · Definition of the promise
The two files are defined distinctly:
File 1 — the question as asked. The user's actual question, verbatim in intent and form. This is what the model must understand in order to know what is being asked of it.
File 2 — the extracted data before redaction. The material the platform has determined is needed to answer the question — the evidence, the records, the context — as extracted, prior to the redaction step being applied to it.
"Treated as unrelated in SyniSense, then linked again post-processing" is the load-bearing mechanic, and it is the owner's explicit ruling. It means:
Each file enters the crossing and is assessed independently — its own de-identification, its own governance checks — with no cross-reference to the other file during that assessment. The assessment of File 1 does not consult File 2, and vice versa.
After each file has been independently processed, the two are re-associated into a single two-item context.
That two-item context is what crosses to the model for one query. The model receives two items and answers the single question they jointly pose.
B4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the organisation experiences: nothing additional and nothing visible — this is an internal mechanic of how the crossing handles a question that needs data. The benefit is indirect: the model receives the question and its supporting data as two clean, independently-governed items, which produces a better-grounded answer than a single pre-merged payload while preserving the independence of each item's governance.
What the external model receives: a two-item context — item one, the governed question; item two, the governed data — presented together as the input to a single query. The model does not receive, and cannot reconstruct, the pre-redaction content of either item; it receives each item in its post-crossing (de-identified) state.
What is produced and retained: the two files are retained under their own custody terms (B5.4), distinct from the per-request de-identification mapping envelope, which — as Document 1 §A5.3 requires — still dies with the call.
B5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
B5.1 · Two items, split before assessment [RULED] A question-with-data request is represented as two distinct files from the point of splitting onward — not one payload that is notionally divided. File 1 (question as asked) and File 2 (extracted data pre-redaction) are separate objects.
B5.2 · Independent assessment, no cross-consultation [RULED] Each file is de-identified and governance-checked on its own. During a file's assessment, the other file is not an input. PART A — PROMISE #6: THE ADAPTIVE, INTENT-MEASURING SECURITY AGENT
A1 · The promise, in plain language
The platform continuously watches everything happening across the estate and the AI it runs — every actor, every input, every action — and asks a question no permission check can answer: not "was this allowed," but "was this actually intended, and is this actor, input, and action what it claims to be?" It detects misuse at machine speed, contains it before a human could wake, learns from every attack it defeats, records everything it does as it does it (including its own mistakes), and proves it still works on a standing schedule without anyone having to check. It is invisible to honest work — the people doing legitimate work never feel it — and it can stop anything while being able to erase nothing.
A2 · The service that powers it
The Watch, through its two engines:
Macho (the eyes) — observes, scores, and asserts facts. It cannot block, throttle, mark, reroute, or notify. Its sole output is a verdict.
Mkono (the hand) — executes signed policy tables against verdicts. It contains no model, no content parsing, no reasoning — a deterministic mapping from verdict properties to actions, small enough to verify exhaustively.
The two meet at exactly one interface: a signed, replayable verdict. The hand never reaches behind that seam to inspect raw behaviour; everything it needs is in the verdict.
A3 · Definition of the promise
The promise decomposes into properties, each of which is separately required:
Totality of sight. No path exists by which an actor, input, or action reaches the estate or a model outside the Watch's observed surface. Every detection claim is meaningless if an unobserved path exists, so this is the first property, not an aspiration.
Detection of the three attack shapes the governors cannot catch: - the machine-speed attack, over before a human wakes — answered by autonomy made safe by construction; - the slow play, drifting beneath every threshold — answered by a stack of constraints (Part IV of this document); - the authorised attack, which never breaks a rule — answered by declared intent reconciled against realised conduct. This is the intent-measuring core and the direct link to Promise #7.
Response at machine speed, safe by construction. The Watch acts autonomously, but every autonomous act is reversible, bounded, and witnessed — so speed does not mean danger.
Invisibility to legitimate use, as a measured number. The share of legitimate actions that experience any Watch friction has a hard ceiling. Invisibility is not an aesthetic claim; it is a budget.
Self-proof on a standing cadence. Every response mechanism demonstrates itself firing on a schedule — full loop, both engines, latencies measured, results as receipts — and a mechanism not demonstrated firing is not in force, whatever any inventory claims.
Records everything, erases nothing. The Watch can stop anything and delete nothing; every action it takes is on the record as it happens, its own mistakes included.
A4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the organisation experiences (human-facing):
The people doing legitimate work do not notice the Watch at all — this is the defining experience, and it is enforced by the friction budget. A conforming actor moves through the platform with lighter verification, not heavier, because their behavioural signature earns them a trust discount.
The security officer (the human accountable for the estate's defence) experiences the Watch as a small number of things genuinely requiring a person: a decision panel of conflicts to resolve, each presented with the evidence and the defined choices. Everything the Watch can safely handle itself, it has already handled — what reaches the person is only what a person must decide.
Where an actor is wrongly caught, they experience an ordinary delay at worst, and — if they were routed into the mirage (Part V) and did work that did not land — they are told plainly that the work did not land, never left to believe a success that did not happen.
What a consuming system experiences (machine-facing):
Every actor, input, and action is observed and, where relevant, carries a verdict. A downstream gate evaluating an actor's standing receives that actor's mark state (Part IV) as a structural fact to check on every pass.
What is produced and retained:
Verdicts (signed, replayable), actions (each citing the policy row that fired), attestations, and marks — all written to the one authoritative record. The Watch mints the first account of its own events and a second account of nobody else's (Document 16 §5).
Standing drill receipts — the self-proof dataset, assembled without human effort, which is what "auditable without much human intervention" means in practice.
A5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
These are the Watch's doctrines and invariants, stated as binding requirements. All are [CANON] unless marked otherwise.
A5.1 · The eyes never act; the hand never judges. Detection asserts facts; response executes policy; one verdict is the only interface between them. A detector that knows what action follows its verdict is tuned defensively and detection quality silently degrades — the documented death of most security operations. Therefore Macho holds no powers whatsoever, and Mkono holds no judgement — a deterministic mapping from verdict properties to actions. This is the platform's standing rule (learning may detect, it may never decide) applied to security whole.
A5.2 · Certainty never exceeds evidence. The Watch's maximum autonomous force (severance) may be unlocked only by signals an outsider cannot fabricate — a closed registry: canary touch, attestation failure, mark hit, witness-write attempt, floor violation, mirage incrimination. A content score or behavioural pattern, however confident, is a signal an attacker can shape; a system whose maximum power can be triggered from outside is a weapon handed to whoever learns to trigger it. The certainty computation is a function with no parameter through which a detector's confidence can enter — the same assertion-boundary discipline Solva uses (Document 4), transplanted here. Everything externally steerable caps at reversible containment, whatever its score.
A5.3 · Every autonomous act is reversible, bounded, and witnessed. A time-to-live on every action; a blast budget on every window; act-and-attest atomically, peer-countersigned. Destroy does not exist in the Watch — deletion remains solely the authority act specified in Document 14. An action that expires unless renewed converts a false positive from a harm into a delay; a budget converts the autoimmune attack (tricking the immune system into suspending the business) from an outage into a bounded cost.
A5.4 · Deployed in force, never in shadow. Every Watch mechanism ships with defined conditions of success and runs at full authority from first deployment. An observe-first ("watching only") mode spends the entire value — response speed — to buy nothing, and is prohibited by doctrine. A5.5 · One record, two readings. The Watch maintains no account of anything but its own events. The compliance owner reads those records as assurance; the security officer reads them as defence; the auditor and the defender can never disagree, because there is one set of facts under both readings. The deception the mirage shows an attacker is a rendering shown outward; the record never lies inward.
A5.6 · Suspicion is refined, not accumulated. Probabilistic detection routes to conditions — friction, narrowed envelopes, the mirage — where the actor's own conduct converts suspicion into certainty or innocence. The Watch does not convict on statistics. Suspicion at eighty percent becomes caught touching a planted credential — zero false positives by construction — and only then does maximum force apply.
A5.7 · Conformance grants trust; non-conformance never convicts. An actor's behavioural signature buys legitimate actors invisibility. Losing conformance returns an actor to baseline verification and nothing more; deviation combines with other signals and is never sole grounds past friction. The worst the signature can ever do to an innocent is make their day ordinary. This asymmetry is constitutional, not tunable.
A5.8 · The floor never learns. Beneath every adaptive layer stands a ruled, deterministic floor, moved only by visible, delayed, two-signature ceremony — and the ceremony is itself a monitored tripwire. The platform does not merely check permissions; it measures whether what an actor is actually doing matches what that actor said they came to do — and it does this continuously, across the whole life of the actor's presence, not once at the door. The Watch, specifically its behavioural strata (Part IV of this document's canon basis): the declared mandate frozen at admission, and the four-layer constraint stack that reconciles conduct against it — the signature, the mark, and the ruled floor beneath the adaptive layers.
B3 · Definition of the promise
"Intent measured across layers" means four distinct reconciliation mechanisms operate together, each defined:
The declared mandate. Every actor — person and agent alike — arrives with a declared mandate: for an agent, an objective with capabilities and risks; for a person, a role and interests. The ruled floor. Beneath every adaptive layer, a deterministic floor that cannot be taught — so the slow play cannot boil it one degree at a time.

Every gate evaluates mark state on every pass. An actor carrying a mark is resolved as marked at admission, invocation, egress, and authentication alike.
What is produced and retained:
The frozen declared mandate (immutable for the actor's admitted presence).
Declared and demonstrated conduct, both as fields of the record, with divergence tracked as the actor acts — not judged once at entry.

PART A — PROMISE #8: GUARDS AGAINST HALLUCINATION
A1 · The promise, in plain language
The platform does not state as fact things it cannot support from real material. When an AI model returns something that cannot be anchored to evidence the platform actually holds, the platform does not pass it on as an answer — a sentence that cannot be grounded is refused, not served as a weaker sentence. And when material genuinely supports only a weak claim ("this was stated" rather than "this is true"), the platform asserts only the weak claim, no matter how confident the model or the reasoning behind it appears. The result is that an answer from the platform can be trusted to sit within what its evidence actually supports.
A2 · The service that powers it
Solva, through its assertion boundary — a mechanical rule set that computes what a conclusion may claim from the governed classes of the units the conclusion rests on, and nothing else. Supported by:
the model ladder and grounding requirement — every model-reaching composition carries a per-sentence anchor map and a lower-rung fallback;
qualification — degraded material is recorded as unqualified and never enters the evidence it would otherwise corrupt.
A3 · Definition of the promise
"Guards against hallucination" is defined as three concrete guarantees:
Grounding is proven, not asserted. A sentence that cannot be anchored to material is not a weaker sentence — it is a refusal. Composed output carries a per-sentence anchor map, and the gate enforces it.
The claim never exceeds the evidence class. A conclusion inherits the floor of its load-bearing units' classes: if any load-bearing unit is utterance-class (someone said it), the conclusion is utterance-class and is asserted as "was stated," never as fact — however overwhelming the evidence behind it is judged to be. Reasoning strength is not an input to this computation.
Degraded material cannot reach consumption. A unit that cannot meet the evidence standard is recorded as unqualified and never enters an evidence partition. Garbage in produces refusal out, not a confident wrong number — and this is architectural, following from qualification, not from anyone noticing a source was degraded.
A4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the organisation experiences (human-facing):
Answers that state facts state facts the platform can stand behind; answers resting on weaker material say so in their own wording ("X was stated" rather than "X is true"). The user is never handed a confident-sounding claim the evidence does not support.
Where an answer cannot be supported at the standard asked, the user receives a refusal with a structured reason — what was asked, the class actually supported, and what would raise it — together with the three things they can actually do about it: accept the result as a recorded statement, narrow the objective, or lower the standard. Nothing else is offered, because nothing else can be delivered.
What a consuming system experiences (machine-facing):
A composed answer arrives with a per-sentence anchor map; a sentence without an anchor is not in the answer. A calling system receives either a grounded answer or a defined refusal, never an ungrounded assertion dressed as an answer.
What is produced and retained:
A trace for every extraction-time judgement (Part C), carrying the reasoning path, the load-bearing units, the class computed, and the conclusion — the record that makes the guard auditable.
A5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
A5.1 · Grounding enforced per sentence [CANON]. Every model-reaching composition carries a per-sentence anchor map, and the gate enforces it. A sentence that cannot be anchored to material is refused. Advisory outputs are marked twice — at write time by the producing service and at render time by the surface — because a marker enforced only at write can be dropped by a renderer that never knew it was load-bearing. (Mark-twice discipline is platform-wide; Document 16 §7.)
A5.2 · The lower-rung fallback, preserved byte-identical [CANON]. Every model-reaching composition carries a lower-rung fallback — the mechanical composer preserved byte-identical as that fallback — because a system whose highest rung has no floor beneath it has a single point at which output quality becomes unbounded. The floor beneath the ladder is always present.
A5.3 · The assertion boundary is parameterless [CANON]. The computation of what a conclusion may claim takes the load-bearing units and nothing else — no confidence, no reasoning strength, no evidence weight. "The evidence is overwhelming, assert the utterance as fact" cannot be expressed, because the function computing the class has no parameter through which reasoning strength could enter. The laundering case is not caught at runtime; it is unrepresentable. A signature assertion in the test suite is what keeps it so.
A5.4 · Qualification is a partition, not a read-time filter [CANON]. A unit that cannot meet the evidence standard is recorded as unqualified and never enters an evidence partition — not filtered at read time (which some path could skip), but excluded from the partition in the first place. This is the mechanism by which degraded material fails to reach consumption.
A5.5 · Refusal is visible and actionable, never a silent downgrade [CANON]. A conclusion whose computed class falls below the objective's floor is refused with a structured reason stating what was asked, the supported class, and what would raise it — refused visibly, never silently downgraded and served. The refusal offers exactly the three actions a person can take (accept as recorded statement, narrow the objective, lower the standard).
A6 · What must be true for the service to deliver as intended
The anchor map is complete and enforced. If a composition can reach a consumer with an unanchored sentence, hallucination has passed the guard.
The assertion boundary's signature stays parameterless. If any change ever adds a confidence or strength parameter to the class computation, the laundering case becomes representable again — the signature assertion test is therefore load-bearing and must run on every build.
The fallback is genuinely byte-identical and always present. A ladder rung without its floor is an unbounded-quality point.
Qualification actually partitions. If unqualified units can reach an evidence partition by any path, "garbage in, refusal out" becomes "garbage in, confident wrong answer out."
PART B — PROMISE #7 (REASONING HALF): INTENT MEASURED WITHIN A REASONING SESSION
B1 · The promise, in plain language
When the platform reasons toward an answer, it does not take the asker's stated purpose at face value and stop there. It records what the asker declared they were doing, and — as the reasoning session unfolds — watches whether the asker's own conduct matches that declaration. A declared search for clarity whose every turn rejects all candidates but the one the asker arrived with is demonstrating confirmation, not inquiry — and the platform tracks that divergence rather than assuming it away. The answer never exceeds the audited intent, and an answer produced under unresolved divergence says so in its own trace.
Scope note. This is the reasoning half of Promise #7 — Solva reading declared-versus-demonstrated intent inside a single reasoning run. The security half — the Watch reconciling an actor's conduct against their frozen mandate across the estate — is in Document 3. The two are distinct: different scope (one run vs. an actor's whole presence), different consequence (reasoning discipline vs. enforcement). Whether they share one divergence-tracking mechanism is held in Document 16 §6.
B2 · The service that powers it
Solva's intent audit — the opening act of a reasoning session, sustained across its stages: it records the situation class the asker declared and reconciles it against the situation class the asker's conduct demonstrates, tracking divergence as the session unfolds.
B3 · Definition of the promise
The declaration is treated as a declaration, never as a truth. Solva's opening audit records what the asker said the situation is; it does not assume that is what the situation is.
Declared and demonstrated intent are both fields of the audit record. Divergence between them is tracked as the session unfolds — not judged once at entry and then forgotten.
The synthesis never exceeds the audited intent. Sustained declared-versus-demonstrated divergence is a trigger on the synthesis gate, beside thin evidence: the surface may reframe honestly in-session, and a synthesis produced under unresolved divergence carries that fact in its trace.
Solva acts on divergence only as reasoning discipline, never as enforcement. Where the asker's admission-time declaration and their demonstrated conduct part ways, that divergence is an emission the Watch reads (Document 3); Solva itself never enforces — it only reasons more carefully.
B4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the organisation experiences:
The asker experiences an answer that stays honest to what they actually asked — the platform does not let a confirmation-seeking line of questioning masquerade as open inquiry and produce an over-strong synthesis. Where divergence is unresolved, the answer itself is honest about it rather than quietly overreaching.
What a consuming system experiences:
A synthesis produced under unresolved divergence carries that marker in its trace, so any downstream consumer of the synthesis can see the condition it was produced under.
The divergence, where it concerns the actor's admission-time mandate, is emitted for the Watch to read — the two authorities share the fact, each acting in its own domain.
What is produced and retained:
The intent-audit record — declared class, demonstrated class, divergence tracked over the session — carried in the reasoning trace (Part C).
B5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
B5.1 · The audit opens the session and is sustained, not one-shot [CANON]. The intent audit is Solva's opening act and runs across the layers that follow; divergence is tracked as the session unfolds, never judged once at entry.
B5.2 · Divergence is a second synthesis-gate trigger [CANON]. Sustained declared-versus-demonstrated divergence triggers the synthesis gate alongside thin evidence. A synthesis produced under unresolved divergence states so in its trace.
B5.3 · Solva reasons on divergence; the Watch enforces [CANON]. Where admission-time declaration (Document 3) and demonstrated conduct part ways, the divergence is an emission the Watch reads and acts on. Solva applies it only as reasoning discipline. The boundary is firm: reasoning quality is Solva's; enforcement is the Watch's; neither performs the other's function.
B6 · What must be true for the service to deliver as intended
The audit genuinely tracks across the session. A one-shot check at entry would miss exactly the drift the promise exists to catch — a session that starts open and narrows to confirmation.
Divergence actually constrains the synthesis. If divergence is recorded but does not gate the synthesis, the audit is decorative.
The Solva/Watch boundary holds both ways. Solva must not enforce (it would become a second security authority); the Watch must receive the emission (or the actor-level picture is blind to session-level conduct). Document 16 §6 holds whether the two divergence mechanisms unify.
PART C — PROMISE #31: THE SOPHISTICATED REASONING TOOL (SOLVA ITSELF)
C1 · The promise, in plain language
The platform holds a genuine reasoning capability — not a pattern-matcher, but a component that reasons the way a careful human mind does: framing a question, weighing candidates, surfacing what contradicts rather than averaging it away, reflecting on what a conclusion actually rests on, and stating a conclusion only as strongly as its grounds allow. This capability can operate on its own or inside another engine's work, and it is applied wherever the system must preserve (keep meaning intact), audit (show why a conclusion holds), defend (justify a conclusion under challenge), or innovate (reason toward something new). It is the seat of judgement in the platform.
C2 · The service that powers it
Solva, the depth governor, in full — its two faculties, its reasoning stages, its assertion boundary, and its trace. Solva "issues operations, interprets results, and governs depth"; it never extracts and never reaches into the operator primitives that do (those belong to Mtafiti, Document 8).
C3 · Definition of the promise
Solva is two faculties joined by a one-way seam:
The reasoning faculty — where genuine judgement happens, through real stages no governed artifact could substitute for (a matrix cannot tell you whether an inference is sound): - Framing — reasoning begins from a declared frame (the intent audit, Part B). - Candidates and Tension — contested material is surfaced, not resolved by averaging: a claim and its retraction, a fact and its correction, are held in view together, because averaging them would produce a conclusion no evidence supports and no reader could trace. - Reflection — produces the load-bearing set: the units the conclusion actually rests on. Identifying which units are load-bearing is itself a genuine reasoning judgement.
The assertion boundary — a purely mechanical rule set (Part A) that reads the governed classes of the load-bearing units and nothing else.
The seam is one-way and is a dependency rule, not a convention: the reasoning faculty informs the composition freely, but its strength — how confident, how well-evidenced it judges its own conclusion — is never an input to the assertion boundary. Reasoning shapes the composition; it can never raise the ceiling on what that composition may claim. Both directions are enforced by import assertion: the assertion module does not import the reasoning faculty's confidence; the reasoning module does not import the class computation.
"Standalone or embedded" [CANON + PROPOSED]: Solva operates at two bars, set by where it operates, both governed by the identical two-faculty structure — only the target differs. This is the canonical basis for the promise's "standalone or as part of another engine" claim: the same reasoning structure applies whether Solva is reasoning toward a delivered answer or judging preservation depth inside extraction.
[RULED] on "innovate." Canon establishes Solva reasoning to preserve, audit, and defend (its stages, its trace, its enforcement are all specified). "Innovate" is Solva's reasoning faculty applied under CIF's governance, not a fourth mode of Solva — the promise's innovate clause is delivered jointly by Solva (the reasoning) and CIF (the governance of ideation), and the CIF interface is specified where CIF's own promises are held.
C4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the organisation experiences:
Where a person uses the platform to think through something hard — an analysis, a defensible position, a genuinely new idea — they experience a counterpart that reasons transparently: it shows what it weighed, holds contradictions in view rather than smoothing them, and states its conclusion at exactly the strength the grounds allow. They can see why it concluded what it did.
What a consuming system / another engine experiences:
An engine that embeds Solva issues a reasoning request and receives an interpreted result plus a trace — never Solva reaching into that engine's own primitives, and never the engine reading Solva's internal confidence past the one-way seam.
What is produced and retained:
The trace (C5) — the auditable record of the reasoning, present from the first commit.
C5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
C5.1 · Two faculties, one-way seam, enforced by import assertion [CANON]. Solva is a free reasoning faculty and a bound assertion boundary. The seam is a dependency rule enforced by import assertion in both directions, not a runtime convention.
C5.2 · Tension surfaces contested material, never averages it [CANON]. Contested material — a claim and its retraction, a fact and its correction — is held in view together. Averaging is prohibited because it produces a conclusion no evidence supports and no reader could trace.
C5.3 · Load-bearing identification returns unit references only [CANON]. The load-bearing function returns unit references and carries no class decision. A load-bearing function that returned a class would collapse the two faculties into one. Solva chooses the units; it does not choose what those units imply.
C5.4 · Construction order: the boundary is built first [CANON]. The assertion boundary is built first, with its parameterless signature, before the reasoning faculty is bound to it. Building the reasoner first and adding the boundary afterward would make the boundary a filter some path could route around.
C5.5 · The floor and class verdict are read-only to Solva [CANON]. At extraction time Solva enforces the objective's defensibility floor and the governed class, both read through read-only handles — Solva reads them, never sets or relaxes them.
C5.6 · The trace exists from the first commit [CANON]. Every extraction-time judgement produces a trace: the reasoning path across its stages, the units found load-bearing, the class computed at the boundary, and the conclusion. The trace is not instrumentation added later — it is the auditability that justifies letting a component reason at all. It carries a trace identifier joining it to the unit-level intelligence and to the record.
C5.7 · Solva governs depth only [CANON]. Direction is Northena (Document 5); boundary is SyniSense (Document 1); depth is Solva. The three axes never collapse into one another — a governor performing another's function would create a single point at which a run could be admitted, bounded, and asserted by the same logic, with no separate check to catch it.
C6 · What must be true for the service to deliver as intended
The one-way seam holds under every change. The single most important property: if reasoning strength ever becomes an input to the assertion boundary, the whole guard against laundering (and against hallucination, Part A) falls. The import assertions are load-bearing tests.
Tension genuinely surfaces rather than resolves. If contested material is averaged anywhere, conclusions become untraceable and unsupported.
The trace is complete and joined to the record. A reasoning capability without a faithful trace is exactly the thing that cannot be allowed to reason — the trace is the permission.
Solva stays in its axis. If Solva ever extracts, or reaches into operator primitives, or enforces (Part B), the axis separation collapses and a single point of compromise appears.
The innovate clause's division with CIF is ruled (C3). Until confirmed, the generative application of Solva is specified only in principle; once ruled, the Solva/CIF interface must be explicit so neither duplicates the other.

The service that powers both promises is Northena, the direction governor. Northena has no separate end-user surface name; it operates beneath the answering and extraction paths. This document specifies the engine.
The organising fact of this document: Northena is deterministic by construction — no model, no learned weights, no adaptive behaviour. It is trustworthy as a governor precisely because it cannot be otherwise. Its behaviour varies enormously run to run, but every part of that variation is driven by a frozen governing artifact it reads, never by Northena adapting. PART A — PROMISE #16: FOCUS MAINTAINED UNDER MASSIVE CONTEXT
A1 · The promise, in plain language
The platform stays on the question it was actually asked, no matter how much data surrounds that question. It does not wander, does not get pulled off-objective by the sheer volume of material available, and does not quietly substitute a different, easier, or larger question for the one it was given. Every run is kept on its objective, brought to a defined stop, and can be shown afterwards to have stayed in scope the whole way — because the thing it was meant to do was fixed before it began and checked at every step against that fixed thing.
A2 · The service that powers it
Northena, through its four-stage loop:
Admit — compiles, validates, and freezes the objective before the run begins.
Gate — tests, at every step, whether a sub-objective falls within the frozen scope, and routes on the result.
Converge — brings the run to a defined stop on conditions fixed in the frozen artifact.
The Ledger — the durable, audit-grade record of how the run was directed.
A3 · Definition of the promise
"Focus maintained under massive context" is defined as three guarantees:
The objective is fixed before the run starts. What the run is meant to achieve — its scope, its stopping condition, its budget, its lawful basis — is compiled and frozen at Admit. Nothing in the volume of surrounding data can change what the run is for, because what it is for was decided and locked before any material was touched.
Every step is checked against the fixed objective. Gate tests scope membership at every routing decision — it checks whether a sub-objective is in the declared scope set, and nothing else. Material outside scope is logged and dropped, not followed. This is what keeps a large context from dragging the run outward: being adjacent and available is not being in scope.
The run stops on a fixed condition, never on a learned one. Converge terminates on the done-condition or the budget, both threshold checks fixed in the frozen artifact. There is no learned stopping — the run does not continue because a model judged there was more interesting material nearby.
A4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the organisation experiences (human-facing):
A person asks a bounded question of a large estate and gets an answer to that question — not a sprawling survey of everything nearby, not a subtly different question the system found easier against the available data. The focus is invisible in the ordinary case: the answer is simply on-point.
Where a run reaches its budget before its done-condition, the person is told the run stopped on budget — a defined, honest stop — rather than being handed a run that quietly kept going past what was commissioned.
What a consuming system experiences (machine-facing):
A commissioned run returns bounded by exactly the scope and budget it was admitted under. A calling system can rely on the run not having exceeded its commission, because the governor — not the executor — enforces the ceiling.
What is produced and retained:
The Ledger record for the run: admission and its outcome, every Gate routing and its reason, every refusal or drop, and the convergence decision — a closed, append-only, audit-grade record. A run with no closed record is an invalid run.
A5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
A5.1 · Deterministic by construction, enforced structurally [CANON]. No Northena module imports a machine-learning library or a learned component, and an import assertion enforcing this runs in continuous integration. Determinism is checked structurally, not by review. The moment a direction governor adjusts gating from past runs, or infers scope probabilistically, it stops being auditable — and auditability is its entire reason to exist.
A5.2 · The accountability standard [CANON]. Every run must be answerable deterministically: admitted because these fields were valid; gated in because scope membership held; stopped because this condition was met; refused because this rule fired. "Admitted because the model scored it highly enough" is the unauditable outcome this standard exists to prevent.
A5.3 · Admit freezes; judgement is borrowed, never performed [CANON]. Admit compiles, validates, and freezes. Where compilation genuinely needs judgement — resolving an ambiguous scope, setting a preservation depth or a floor — Admit invokes the depth governor (Solva, Document 4) through an opaque handle and freezes the value it returns. The judgement is the reasoner's; the freeze is Northena's alone. Northena never reads that governor's internals and never performs inference itself.
A5.4 · Gate is strict set-membership, with no inferential path [CANON]. Gate tests whether a sub-objective falls within the scope the frozen artifact declares — membership against a defined set, and nothing else. Ambiguous membership is a compilation defect resolved at Admit by the reasoner, never a Gate-time judgement. No inferential path exists in Gate. This is the specific mechanism by which a massive context cannot pull the run off-objective: scope is a frozen set, and adjacency is not membership.
A5.5 · Converge owns the halt on fixed conditions [CANON]. Converge decides termination on two conditions fixed in the frozen artifact, both threshold checks: done-condition met terminates as success; budget exhausted terminates on budget; neither continues. There is no learned stopping. The depth governor may report it cannot reason further soundly — that report is an input Converge acts on, but the stop decision and its record belong to Northena alone, because a component that could stop a run without Northena recording why would put a termination outside the audit surface entirely.
A5.6 · The budget is a hard stop [CANON]. A run halts at its ceiling and nothing past it is billed. This is what makes an open-ended run commissionable at all: exposure is bounded at commit, by a number the commissioner set, enforced by the governor rather than by the executor that could be tempted to keep going.
A6 · What must be true for the service to deliver as intended
The determinism import-assertion runs on every build. If Northena could import a learned component, it could adapt its gating, and the accountability standard would silently fail. The assertion is load-bearing.
Ambiguity is resolved at Admit, never at Gate. If Gate ever decides membership by inference, the frozen scope stops being the thing that bounds the run, and a large context can pull it outward again.
Converge is the sole owner of the halt. If anything else can stop a run without Northena recording why, a run can end outside the audit surface.
The budget ceiling is enforced by the governor, not the executor. An executor that enforces its own ceiling can be tempted past it; the governor cannot.
PART B — PROMISE #18 (DIRECTION HALF): OBJECTIVE PRESERVATION
B1 · The promise, in plain language
What a run was asked to do cannot be quietly changed into something else while the run is happening. The objective is frozen before the run begins and is immutable for the life of the run; if the intent genuinely changes, that is a new run with a new frozen objective, never an edit of a live one. This means the thing a run's result is judged against is the same thing the run was commissioned to do — nobody can move the target after the fact to match whatever came back.
Scope note. Promise #18 has two halves. This document specifies the direction half — Northena freezing and preserving a run's objective. The judgement-retention half — CIF governing how the system's own judgements are retained and improved across the build loop, the training path, planning, and proposal generation — belongs to CIF and is specified where CIF's promises are held. The two are distinct: one preserves a single run's objective; the other preserves and improves the system's accumulated judgement. They are not folded together here.
B2 · The service that powers it
Northena's Admit stage and its freeze, together with the append-only Ledger that makes the frozen objective and every decision against it a permanent, inspectable record.
B3 · Definition of the promise
The freeze is atomic and the artifact immutable for the run. At Admit, the governing artifact is frozen. It carries its type and identity, its version, the scope set Gate will test membership against, the defensibility floor, the done-condition, the budget, and the lawful basis. Once frozen, none of these can change for the run.
A changed intent is a new admission, never an edit. If what is being pursued changes, a new artifact is admitted and frozen. There is no path that edits a live objective, because such a path would let the target move mid-run.
The record is a contract, not a log. The Ledger is frozen, versioned, append-only, and closed on every run. A correction is a new entry referencing what it corrects, never an edit of what was written. Refusals and drops are recorded exactly as successes are — a system that drops what it declined cannot show what it declined.
B4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the organisation experiences:
A commissioned run's purpose is stable: the person who commissioned it, and an auditor later, both see the same fixed objective and can confirm the result addresses it. Where the need changed partway, they see a clean new run, not a silently mutated old one.
What a consuming system experiences:
An external request arrives complete or is refused; a request missing any required field is refused with the validation envelope, and it carries an idempotency key so a retried submission neither double-commissions nor double-charges. The frozen objective the run executes under is the same one referenced in the record.
What is produced and retained:
The frozen governing artifact and the closed Ledger record — together, the permanent proof of what the run was for and how it was directed.
B5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
B5.1 · The freeze is atomic; the artifact is immutable for the run [CANON]. A changed intent is a new admission, never an edit of a live one. The frozen artifact carries type and identity, version, scope set, defensibility floor, done-condition, budget, and lawful basis.
B5.2 · Lawful basis is enforced at Admit, before any extraction [CANON]. Purpose limitation is an admission property, not a downstream filter — a run with no lawful basis never starts, so no material is ever touched under an unstated basis. Five deterministic validity checks run in order at Admit, each with its own refusal reason; a failure at any point refuses the admission and writes the reason, and no downstream stage runs.
B5.3 · Two admission paths, both freezing the same way [CANON]. An external request is checked and frozen at Admit having arrived complete; a work order cannot freeze until the shaping conversation completes the objective envelope — the shaping dialogue is where the envelope is filled, Admit is where it is checked and frozen. Both paths write a Ledger row carrying the artifact reference and the lawful-basis reference. This is enforced as code: the ledger writer exposes append and nothing else, checked by import assertion. Out-of-scope and refused items are recorded, never silently dropped.
B5.5 · The stamp-audit side-channel is deliberately permissive [CANON]. The Ledger absorbs untyped side-channel entries from other engines — boundary emissions, measurement stamps, trace correlation, gate receipts, refusal envelopes — without mutating the frozen row contract. Typing it would harden it into a mutation surface; the permissiveness is guarded by a snapshot test so the decision cannot be quietly reversed. Unit and audit stay in separate envelopes with separate lifecycles, joined by identity rather than embedded.
B6 · What must be true for the service to deliver as intended
No edit path to a frozen artifact exists. If a live objective can be edited, the target can be moved to match the result, and the run's record proves nothing.
The append-only property is enforced as code, not policy. If the ledger writer exposes any update or delete path, corrections become indistinguishable from rewrites of history.
Refusals and drops are recorded. A record that shows only successes cannot demonstrate what was declined, which is a substantial part of what the record is for.
The direction half and the CIF judgement-retention half stay distinct. Objective preservation for a run (here) and judgement retention for the system (CIF) must not be conflated, or one engine's guarantees get incorrectly assumed of the other.

Direction, Scope & Focus — Part 2: Progressive Disclosure
Document 6 of 16 · Canonical statement of requirements
Cluster: Direction, Scope & Focus

Standalone statement of what must be true. Shared mechanisms are named and Document 16 cited; each requirement stated in full here.

The framing that governs this entire document [RULED]. Progressive disclosure is a backend capability before it is anything a person sees. The failure it prevents happens to agents and processing steps, and it happens before a human ever asks a question: an engine, model call, or processing step handed the whole estate — or the whole context accumulated so far — degrades the same way a person shown everything at once would, except nothing catches it, because there is no human judgement in that step to notice the confusion. This document specifies that backend discipline. Any human-facing consequence of it is downstream of this mechanism, not its source.
The services that power this promise are the platform's direction governor (Northena, Document 5) and its memory/serving layer (Document 8) acting together, plus a disclosure discipline stated here as its own requirement set.
PART A — THE PROMISE
A1 · The promise, in plain language
No agent, model call, or processing step is ever handed more context than it needs to do the specific thing in front of it. The platform serves the minimal-sufficient slice of material for the task at hand and expands it only on demand, so that a step doing a bounded job is not confused, diluted, or derailed by the mass of everything else the estate holds. This protection operates whether or not any human is involved — it protects the machine consumers of context first, because they are the ones who encounter the data before a person asks anything, and they are the ones who cannot notice when too much has buried the signal.
A2 · The service that powers it
Three mechanisms acting together:
Northena's scope discipline (Document 5) — the frozen scope set that already bounds what a run may touch. Progressive disclosure operates within an in-scope run: even material that is in scope is not all handed to a step at once.
The memory/serving layer (Document 8) — which serves distilled, qualified intelligence rather than raw estate, and can fetch more specific material on demand (the "traffic controller"). Progressive disclosure is the discipline that governs how much it serves to any one consumer at a time.
The disclosure discipline (this document) — the requirement that context be assembled minimal-first and expanded on demand, with the expansion itself governed.
A3 · Definition of the promise
"Progressive disclosure" as a backend capability is defined as four guarantees:
Minimal-sufficient first. For any task handed to an agent, model call, or processing step, the context assembled for it is the smallest set of material sufficient to do that task — not everything in scope, not everything related, not everything accumulated so far.
Expansion is on demand and bounded. Where a step genuinely needs more — it cannot complete the task on the minimal slice — it requests more, and the platform serves the next increment. Expansion is a governed request, not an unbounded pour: a step cannot silently pull the whole estate by asking once.
The consumer is protected whether or not a human is present. The discipline applies to autonomous steps exactly as it would to a person. An agent chain, a reasoning run, a model call, an extraction worker — each is served minimally and expands on demand. There is no "no human here, so give it everything" path.
Disclosure state is attributable. What was disclosed to a given step, and what it subsequently requested, is recorded — so that an over-broad disclosure, or a step that requested far more than its task warranted, is visible after the fact rather than silent.
A4 · Delivery experience expectations

The human-facing benefit is a consequence of the backend discipline, not a separate feature: because agents and steps were kept focused, the answers and intelligence that reach people are sharper and better-grounded, and the system is less prone to the confident wandering that an over-fed model produces. A disclosure record per step: what minimal slice was served, what expansions were requested and served, joined to the run's record — so disclosure breadth is auditable.

The services that power this promise are Solva (the depth governor, whose trace makes an extracted artifact self-describing) and the platform's two-service architecture — an asset-building service and an objective-serving service — whose boundary is the normalised unit. This document specifies how richness is preserved when the estate is distilled from raw material into the much smaller body of qualified intelligence the platform actually serves from.
The organising fact of this document. Extraction is a shrinking operation: a large raw estate becomes a much smaller body of qualified intelligence. The promise is that this shrink is bounded, not lossy — that what survives the shrink still carries enough of its meaning, its provenance, and the objective it was extracted under that it can be understood, trusted, and re-interpreted later, rather than being a compressed residue whose context has been thrown away. The mechanism that makes the shrink bounded rather than lossy is that every surviving artifact describes itself.
PART A — THE PROMISE
A1 · The promise, in plain language
When the platform reduces a large body of data down to the intelligence it actually needs — which it must, because reasoning over the whole raw estate on every question is neither affordable nor focused — the meaning does not leak out in the reduction. What survives carries with it what it is, how good it is, what it was drawn from, and what question it was answering, so that a later reader — human or machine — can trust it, trace it back, and re-use it for a new question without having to re-process the entire original source. Shrinking the data does not shrink the context; the context travels with the intelligence.
A2 · The service that powers it
Solva's trace and the normalised unit's five rings — every extracted unit carries content, provenance, relational edges, context, and defensibility, and every extraction-time judgement carries a trace. Together these are what make a shrunken artifact self-describing.
The re-extraction handle — carried by every unit, this is what turns provenance from a label into a live capability: a unit can be regenerated from its source and compared against what is currently held.
A3 · Definition of the promise
"Context richness preservation on shrinkage" is defined as four guarantees:
Every surviving unit is self-describing. A unit of qualified intelligence carries five rings — content, provenance, relational edges, context, and defensibility — so that the unit alone tells a reader what it is, where it came from, how it relates to other units, the circumstances it was extracted under, and how strongly it may be relied upon. Nothing essential about the unit lives only in the discarded raw source.
Every judgement is traceable. Every extraction-time judgement produces a trace: the reasoning path, the units found load-bearing, the class computed, and the conclusion. The trace is what lets a reader see why a conclusion was reached and why it was asserted at the strength it was — the reasoning survives the shrink, not just the result.
Provenance is a live handle, not a dead label. Every unit carries a re-extraction handle that resolves to the exact position in the source it came from — a timestamp span in audio, a page and region in a document — so that the unit can be regenerated from its source and compared against what is held. Provenance you can act on is what makes the shrink reversible where it needs to be.
What could not be preserved at standard is refused, not silently degraded. A unit that cannot meet the evidence standard is recorded as unqualified and never enters an evidence partition. The shrink never produces a confident-looking unit whose context was actually lost — such material is marked unqualified rather than admitted at a guessed strength.
A4 · Delivery experience expectations
What a consuming system / later run experiences (the primary consumer):
A later run answering a new question against already-extracted intelligence receives units that are self-describing — it does not need the original raw source to understand what it has, because each unit carries its own content, provenance, context, and defensibility. This is what makes the small, distilled body of intelligence genuinely re-usable rather than a lossy summary.
Where a later run needs to verify or refresh a unit, it uses the re-extraction handle to regenerate that unit from source and compare — it does not re-process the whole estate to check one thing.
What the organisation experiences (human-facing):
An auditor or analyst examining a conclusion can see the trace behind it — the reasoning path, the load-bearing units, the class computed — and can follow any unit back to the precise place in the source it came from. The richness that justifies trusting the conclusion is present and inspectable, even though the raw source is no longer being carried in working context.
What is produced and retained:
Self-describing units (five rings each, with re-extraction handles) and traces (one per extraction-time judgement), joined by a shared trace identifier to the record. These are the durable, richness-preserving artifacts the shrink produces.
A5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
A5.1 · The five-ring unit is a frozen contract [CANON]. The normalised unit carries five rings — content, provenance, relational edges, context, and defensibility — and admits structured sources exactly as it admits perceived ones: a structured record becomes a unit carrying a text modality and a free locator, with no second intake contract anywhere in the platform. The unit is the frozen interface between the asset-building service and the objective-serving service.
A5.2 · Every unit carries a re-extraction handle [CANON]. Without it, provenance is a label; with it, a unit can be regenerated from its source and compared against what is currently held. For non-text material the handle resolves to a position in the source — a timestamp span in audio, a page and region in a document — which is what makes provenance real for perceived material rather than a pointer to the file the unit lives in.
A5.3 · Qualification partitions degraded material out [CANON]. A unit that cannot meet the evidence standard is recorded as unqualified and never enters an evidence partition — not a read-time filter some path could skip, but a partition the material never enters. This is what prevents the shrink from producing confident residue whose context was lost: garbage does not become a confident unit; it becomes an unqualified one.
A5.4 · The trace exists from the first commit and joins to the record [CANON]. Every extraction-time judgement produces a trace carrying its reasoning path, its load-bearing units, and its computed class. The trace is not instrumentation added later — it is the auditability that justifies letting a component reason at all — and it carries a trace identifier joining it to the unit-level intelligence and to the record (Document 4, Document 5). Unit and audit stay in separate envelopes with separate lifecycles, joined by identity rather than embedded, so a unit and its trace can be retained, exported, and expired independently.
A5.5 · The trace is the executed reasoning, never a post-hoc narrative [RULED]. The trace must record the reasoning stages that were actually executed — the frame, the candidates, the tension surfaced, the reflection, the load-bearing set — not a summary written about the reasoning after the fact. The moment a trace becomes a narrative composed to describe a conclusion rather than the executed path that produced it, context preservation becomes context fabrication: the artifact would appear self-describing while describing something that did not happen. This is a genuine engineering constraint on how the trace is produced, and it is load-bearing for the promise's honesty.
[RULED] Adopted, as an explicit statement of a property the canon trace implies but did not state in these words. The promise ("richness preserved") is only true if the preserved richness is faithful; a fabricated trace satisfies the letter of "self-describing" while defeating its purpose.
A5.6 · The transform reads stored intelligence, never a running tail [CANON]. Transformation into a delivered output reads stored qualified intelligence, never the live tail of a mining run — because transforming from a running tail would produce an output whose composition depends on exactly when it was taken, which is not a property any deliverable should have. The shrink's product is stable material, not a moving one.
A6 · What must be true for the service to deliver as intended
Every unit genuinely carries all five rings. A unit missing its context or defensibility ring is not self-describing, and the shrink has lost exactly what the promise says it preserves.
The re-extraction handle genuinely resolves to source position. A handle that points only to a file, not a position within it, cannot regenerate the specific unit — provenance is back to being a label, and the shrink is no longer reversible where it needs to be.
Qualification actually partitions. If degraded material can enter an evidence partition, the shrink produces confident units whose context was lost — the precise failure the promise forbids.
The trace is faithful to executed reasoning (A5.5). If the trace can be a post-hoc narrative, self-description becomes fabrication, and the richness is preserved in appearance only. This is the single most important honesty condition for the promise.
Unit and trace remain separately lifecycled but identity-joined. If they are embedded rather than joined, they cannot be retained, exported, or expired independently, and the separation of output from audit trail is lost.

B0 · The governing distinction: economizers fail transparent; custody fails closed
A custody control (the crossing, Document 1) fails closed — if it cannot do its job, the operation does not happen, because the risk of proceeding is exposure. LC-1 serves a cached result only where the question, its frozen objective, its scope, and its governing rules are identical to the prior — a match on the governed request, not merely on surface text. A different scope, a changed rule, or a staler estate is a cache miss, not a stale hit.
B1.2 [PROPOSED]. LC-1 is distinct from warm-serving (Document 8). Warm/fresh is estate-level — has this data been mined. LC-1 is query-level — has this exact governed question been answered. They compose (a warm-served answer may still be cache-served on identical repeat) and must not be built as one mechanism. (Recorded in Document 16 as a composition, so the two are not conflated.)
B1.3 [PROPOSED]. LC-1 fails transparent: a cache failure produces a fresh computation, never a wrong or refused answer.
B1.4 [PROPOSED]. Cache invalidation follows the estate's own freshness (Document 9): when a region's measure changes (a retraction, a re-census), any cached result depending on that region is invalidated, because a cached answer that outlived the truth it rested on would be a correctness failure, not a saving.
B2 · LC-2 — Model Routing by Task Class
Definition. Different task classes need different model power; routing each task class to the least powerful model that meets its standard reduces cost without lowering quality.
Requirements:
B2.1 [PROPOSED]. LC-2 is the ladder's selection rule (A5.1) applied at the granularity of task class: each class routes to the lowest rung that meets its promise, and the placement is inspectable in the registry.
B2.2 [PROPOSED]. The routing decision is governed by measured performance of each rung on each task class on real material — not by a fixed assumption about which model suits which task. A rung is used for a class only where it has been measured to meet that class's standard.
B2.3 [PROPOSED]. LC-2 fails transparent and upward: if the routed rung cannot meet the standard on a given item, the item escalates to a higher rung (the cascade shape, A5.4), never returns a sub-standard answer.
B3 · LC-3 — Retrieval Narrowing
Definition. Sending a model only the material it needs, rather than everything available, reduces token cost and improves focus.
Requirements:
B3.1 [RULED, = Document 6]. LC-3 is the same discipline as progressive disclosure (Document 6) and the memory layer's on-demand fetch (Document 8): minimal-sufficient context first, expanded on demand. It is not a separate mechanism — it is the cost-facing name for the serving discipline those documents specify. This is recorded explicitly in Document 16 so retrieval narrowing, progressive disclosure, and on-demand fetch are built as one serving discipline, never three.
B3.2 [PROPOSED]. LC-3 narrows within scope and custody: narrowing never drops material the objective requires to be correct (that would change the answer, forbidden by B0), and never reaches material the frozen scope does not admit.
B4 · The Cost Ledger
Definition. The levers need one accountable measure of whether they are working.
B4.1 [PROPOSED]. The Cost Ledger's one job is to show that tokens-per-answer falls. It records, per answer, the external-token cost incurred and the cost that would have been incurred without the levers, so the saving is measured, not asserted — consistent with A5.3 (cost is always measured). A lever that cannot demonstrate a saving in the Cost Ledger is a lever to reconsider, not a capability to assume.
B4.2 [RULED]. The priority order among cost mechanisms, where several could apply, is: cache (LC-1) → route by task class (LC-2) → narrow retrieval (LC-3) → cascade/compression → waste report. Cache is checked first because it is free to check. Routing precedes narrowing because narrowing can be more precise once the model tier is known — a weaker model may need more spelled out, a frontier model less, so route-then-narrow is model-aware rather than one-size-fits-all. Cascade follows narrowing because canon already gates it to "demonstrated cost pressure only" — the specialised mechanism earns its place after the general levers have done what they can. Waste report is last because it is not a lever at all: it is the post-call measurement step that feeds back into tuning the other four.
B5 · What must be true for the levers to deliver as intended
No lever ever changes an answer (B0). The single constitutional property: a lever alters cost, never correctness or exposure. A lever that could change an answer has become a decision-maker and must be removed or re-classed.
The token-preservation invariant holds across every lever. A lever that mutated a de-identification token would breach custody; the build must fail on it.
Every lever's saving is measured in the Cost Ledger. A lever assumed to save but not measured is exactly the unmet cost claim that damages trust.
LC-3, progressive disclosure, and on-demand fetch are one mechanism. If built as three, a consumer narrowed on one path is unprotected on another, and three divergent implementations drift. Document 16 §9 holds this.
LC-1 invalidates on estate freshness. A cached answer that outlives the truth it rested on is a correctness failure wearing a saving's clothes.

B4.2 [RULED] — Lever priority order confirmed: cache → route → narrow → cascade → waste report, with reasoning at B4.2 above. Settled.
Document 16 §§9–10 (composition) — LC-1 vs. warm-serving (distinct, composed); LC-3 = progressive disclosure = on-demand fetch (one mechanism). Both recorded there so the levers are neither conflated with, nor duplicated against, the serving mechanisms.
The levers are ruled in and specified in full; the open items are confirmations of shape and ordering, not questions of whether the levers exist.

PART A — PROMISE #10: THE PULL SEAM — COMPUTE WITHOUT MOVING DATA OUT
A1 · The promise, in plain language
The platform connects to compute power — GPUs and other accelerators — and runs its work on that compute without the data leaving the platform to get there. The compute is brought to the data, not the data to the compute. Whether the work runs on the organisation's own hardware or on capacity the platform allocates, it runs inside the organisation's perimeter, and the estate never egresses to be processed.
A2 · The service that powers it
The deployment topology (Document 1 §6) — the platform installs inside the organisation's perimeter, and compute does not leave it.
The perception execution seam (Document 9) — the same pipeline runs on general-purpose or accelerated hardware behind one seam, attributable per result, without changing the result contract.
Fleet allocation — a versioned policy that apportions capacity across workloads, managed at the control surface.
A3 · Definition of the promise
Compute runs inside the perimeter. Models the organisation hosts, and GPU capacity the platform allocates, both run inside the organisation's own infrastructure and network boundary. Data is processed where it lives.
The data does not move to reach compute. Connecting compute does not create a path by which estate data leaves the perimeter. The only path that leaves the perimeter is the custody boundary (the external-model crossing); compute connection is not such a path.
Hardware is invisible to the consumer. Which hardware ran a piece of work is recorded (so a mixed fleet is attributable) but is never surfaced to the requester and never changes the shape of what the work returns. The requester sees delivery time, not hardware.
A4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the organisation experiences (human-facing):
The organisation connects compute — its own, or capacity the platform allocates — and its data stays inside its perimeter throughout processing. It never faces a choice between using accelerated compute and keeping custody of its data; it gets both. It sees delivery time as the capacity signal, never raw hardware numbers.
What a consuming system experiences (machine-facing):
Work executes against connected compute through one seam; the result records which execution mode ran it, and the result contract is identical regardless of hardware — so no downstream consumer needs to know about hardware, and a mixed fleet is attributable rather than ambiguous.
What is produced and retained:
Per result, the execution-mode attribution (which hardware class ran it); and, at the control surface, the fleet-allocation policy (versioned) that apportions capacity.
A5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
A5.1 · Compute does not leave the perimeter [CANON]. The platform installs inside the organisation's data centre, private cloud, or own cloud tenancy. Models the organisation hosts, and GPU capacity the platform allocates, run inside the perimeter. Exactly one path leaves the perimeter — the custody boundary — and compute connection is not that path.
A5.2 · The execution seam keeps the result contract hardware-independent [CANON]. The same pipeline runs on general-purpose or accelerated hardware behind one seam; each result records which. The result contract is unchanged by execution mode — hardware is an implementation fact, and if it changed the shape of what work returns, every downstream consumer would have to know about hardware, which the seam exists to prevent.

No compute path egresses estate data. If connecting compute could move data outside the perimeter to be processed, the custody guarantee (Document 1) is breached by the compute layer, and the promise is false.

PART B — PROMISE #11: COST AT THE POINT OF REFLECTION, BEFORE COMMIT
B1 · The promise, in plain language
Before the platform commits to doing a piece of work, it tells you what that work will cost — at the moment you are deciding whether to proceed, not after the fact. When the platform reflects your objective back to you for confirmation, the cost of that objective is part of what it reflects, so no commitment is ever made, and no charge ever raised, without the cost having been shown first and the exposure bounded by a number you set.
B2 · The service that powers it
The shaping-and-freeze flow — the objective is shaped with the requester, and at freeze (commit) the feasibility verdict, the cost, and the budget are presented before the objective is committed.
The budget ceiling (Document 5) — a hard stop enforced by the governor, so exposure is bounded at commit by a number the requester set.
Sampling — a first-class narrow objective that converts an unknown-cost case into measured evidence before the full commit.
B3 · Definition of the promise
Cost is shown at the point of commit, before commit. At the freeze step — the reflection where the objective is confirmed — the requester sees the feasibility verdict, the cost, the budget, and the lawful basis, and only then commits. Nothing is committed or charged before this.
Exposure is bounded by a number the requester set. The budget is a hard stop: a run halts at its ceiling and nothing past it is billed. This is what makes an open-ended run commissionable — the requester's own ceiling bounds the exposure, enforced by the governor rather than the executor.
Every extension re-quotes. Extending a running objective generates a new quote for the addition rather than folding silently into the existing commitment, because one commitment covering two different scopes cannot be reconciled afterward.
Where cost is genuinely unknown, it is converted to evidence, not guessed. Feasibility honestly returns unknown on un-censused reach; a sample — a first-class narrow objective — converts that unknown into observed volume, class distribution, and per-hour cost before the full objective is committed. A sample result is evidence, not a promise.
B4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the organisation experiences (human-facing):
At the moment of deciding whether to run an objective, the requester sees its cost and sets its budget — the reflection that confirms the objective also prices it. They are never surprised by a charge after the fact. Where the cost cannot be known in advance (the reach is un-censused), they can run a bounded sample first and see real numbers before committing the full run, with the sample's own cost shown drawing against the budget.
A commissioned run cannot exceed the budget the requester set; it halts at the ceiling. Extending it produces a fresh quote, never a silent addition.

PART B — PROMISE #22: AUTONOMOUS TRAINING, ANY OUTPUT DIMENSION
B1 · The promise, in plain language
The platform can train models on its own — running the training job through to a finished, usable model without a person having to shepherd each step — and the model it produces is trustworthy because it had to prove itself against a fixed bar to exist at all. The autonomy is safe because the acceptance test is automatic and cannot be waved through: the model meets every criterion, on uncurated held-out material, or it does not enter service.
B2 · The service that powers it
Targeta — which selects, from the estate, what to train on and in what order, with its learning walled from what the objective may reach.
The training discipline — the six-criterion acceptance, the recipe seat, and the foundation methods fixed as canon.
Blinded comparison — where two candidates compete, scored blind to prevent identity leaking through form.
B3 · Definition of the promise
Acceptance is automatic and non-negotiable — six criteria, no person in the path. A trained model is accepted into the registry, deployed into extraction, or delivered to a customer only when all six criteria hold: it beats its base on the target stratum, damages nothing else beyond a bound, evaluates on held-out uncurated material, carries complete lineage with inherited rights, calibrates, and ships with an evaluation card. None is a review meeting; all six are artefacts the training run itself produces. A run that cannot produce them was not a governed training run.
Base weights do not change; adaptation is bounded and versioned per instance. This lets capability replicate across instances without forking a core that would then need parallel maintenance forever.
Lineage is visible, including failure. A rejected version stays on the shelf permanently with its reason; resubmission creates the next version in the same lineage and re-runs every check — surfacing failure is what makes an accepted version's numbers mean anything.
Training material is qualified, de-duplicated, and rights-clean. Training draws exclusively from de-duplicated, cleaned, qualified units (Documents 7, 9, 10), and every base model is registered with its hash and licence.
B4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the user/analyst experiences (human-facing):
The analyst watches a training run as staged progress with the current stage live, the candidate's error against its base as checkpoints land, and each acceptance check moving from pending to measuring to settled with its real number attached — a training batch that trips a rail holds inline, visibly, rather than behind a spinner. They see lineage, including what failed and why.
The user does not have to manually judge or approve the model into service — acceptance is the six criteria, automatically. What they get is a model that provably cleared the bar, or a recorded rejection with its numbers.
What a consuming system experiences (machine-facing):
A model enters the registry only with its six acceptance artefacts and its lineage (recipe version, base model, rights). A result produced under a given model records the registry entry it ran under, so it stays interpretable as the registry grows.
What is produced and retained:
The accepted model with its six acceptance artefacts, evaluation card, and lineage; rejected candidates retained permanently with their reasons; and the recipe version that trained each.
B5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
B5.1 · The six acceptance criteria are structural, with no human in the path [CANON]. All six hold or the model does not enter the registry, is not deployed, and is not delivered. They are checked as a set of cells over files the training run already produced; no person sits in that path.
B5.2 · Targeta learns yield but never narrows reach [CANON]. Targeta is the one component permitted to learn, and its yield layer is structurally confined: it receives only the floor-passing eligible set, outputs a permutation of exactly that set (none added, none removed — a non-permutation raises), and never receives the floor value, the raw measure, or the eligibility logic. Improving efficiency by starving a class fails Targeta's admission gate regardless of the efficiency gained — the veto exists precisely because the most effective way for a learned ranker to cut work is to stop visiting classes that pay off rarely, which is the silent narrowing the design forbids. On gate failure, Targeta runs on its deterministic core alone.
B5.3 · Foundation methods and material are fixed [CANON]. Adapter methods on registered open base models; training material drawn exclusively from de-duplicated, cleaned, qualified units; acceptance exclusively through the six criteria on uncurated held-out sets. Base weights do not change; adaptation is bounded and versioned per instance.
B5.4 · The recipe seat is governed and evidence-bound [CANON]. All training configuration lives in a versioned, ledgered configuration object; the seat ships empty, with no default recipe until the first real run fills it. Every trained model's registry entry cites the recipe version that trained it. Recipe revisions carry no approval ceremony but must attach the measured comparison that justifies them and write a ledger row — a revision with no evidence is a defect, not a shortcut.
B5.5 · Candidate comparison is blinded [CANON]. Where two candidates are compared, the comparison is scored blind: arm identity concealed at scoring, outputs in randomised order normalised to remove identifying artefacts, identity unsealed only after scores are fixed, and a leakage check run before unsealing — because identity leaks through form, ordering, and length as readily as through a name.
B5.6 · Retained learning data from exceptional runs [RULED — see Document 14]. The promise-set's #25 (retained learning data usable for white papers) draws on the lineage and evaluation artefacts every run already produces. Evidence crosses between authorities only through the record matrix, provenance-marked with the identity that produced it, never sideways through a dual-hatted task. Workers serve authorities across time and never within a task. This is the mechanism of "no context spillage."
B5.3 · The objective is frozen against recalibration [CANON]. The job runs under a frozen objective (Northena, Document 5); a changed intent is a new admission. This is the mechanism of "no objective recalibration in the delivery cycle."
B5.4 · The path of success is the composition of contracts for a job class [RULED — best practice, per job class]. A job class's path of success is defined as the ordered set of stages and the handoff contract governing each crossing between them. The path is explicit — held as the composition of Mkondo contracts for that job class — so that "the path of success for a job" is a specified, auditable object, not an emergent behaviour.
[RULED] Per job class, not per individual job — the handoff contracts are properties of the service crossings, which are the same for every job of a class; a per-job path would re-specify the same crossings repeatedly. Per-job refinement can be added later where a specific job genuinely needs a non-standard path.
B6 · What must be true for the promise to deliver as intended
Every crossing is genuinely a registered contract. An unregistered handoff that is tolerated rather than flagged is exactly the context-spillage path the promise forbids.
No dual-hatted task exists to leak context sideways. If a task could act under two identities, evidence could cross outside the matrix and context would spill — the single-identity rule (Part A, Mkondo) is what prevents it.
The objective is genuinely immutable for the run. If the objective could be edited mid-delivery, recalibration would be possible and the promise false.
The path of success is a specified object (B5.4). If the path is emergent rather than the defined composition of contracts, "defines the path of success" is not actually delivered.
PART C — PROMISE #27: RULE BOOKS AND TEMPLATES, WRITTEN AND CHANGED BY CEREMONY
C1 · The promise, in plain language
The rules that govern the estate can be written, adopted from templates, and edited in plain human language by the people accountable for them — and any change to a rule happens only in the open, through a defined ceremony: someone proposes it, a second person signs off, a visible countdown runs during which it can still be cancelled, then it applies and is re-verified. Rules never change quietly or on one person's say-so, and the rules that protect the security service itself change on the slowest, most-watched ceremony of all.
C2 · The service that powers it
The rule-and-declaration machinery — rules authored in plain language, sorted by subject and by how binding they are.
The governance-change ceremony — propose → counter-sign → visible countdown → apply → re-verify — the same shape as the authorised-deletion act, because a rule change and a deletion are both consequential changes to a governance value.
C3 · Definition of the promise
Rules are plain language, authored and template-adoptable [CANON]. Rules read as plain sentences ("Business Development Officers may not see payroll data"), each showing who set it, who approved it, and when; sorted by subject and by bindingness (a hard line that can never be switched off; an ordinary rule that changes only through approval; a permission rule; a managed list where adding is immediate but removing needs sign-off). Templates (external rulebooks and frameworks the estate is held to) can be adopted and then edited in the same plain language.
Changes run the ceremony, in the open [CANON]. A rule changes only by: proposal → a second signature → a visible countdown ("takes effect in N hours — you can still cancel") → application → re-verification. Hard lines have no change path at all. Any change to the security service's own rules runs on the longest countdown and is flagged for watching the moment it is raised.
The ceremony is the same shape as authorised deletion [CANON]. Deletion — the most consequential change a governance value can undergo — carries dual control and a waiting period during which it can be cancelled, ending in a destruction attestation generated from the record. A rule change and a deletion share this shape deliberately: both are governed changes to governance values.
C4 · Delivery experience expectations
What the accountable person experiences (human-facing) [RULED — backend states, not screen design]:
The Data Protection Officer authors and edits rules in plain language and adopts templates, working through defined operations: propose a change, which enters it into the ceremony; a second authorised person signs off; a countdown runs, cancellable; the change applies; it is re-verified. Each operation is a defined state transition on a rule. (Consistent with the backend-only scope of this requirements pass, these are specified as states and transitions, not as screen layouts.)
The person sees the whole change on the record: the old wording and the new side by side, both signatures, the countdown as it ran, when it applied, and the re-verification result.
What a consuming system experiences (machine-facing):
A rule's state (in force / proposed / counting down / applied / re-verified / cancelled) is a defined, recorded state; every gate that enforces the rule reads the version in force, and every run records which rule version was in force at the time — so "was this term protected on a given date" always has an answer.
What is produced and retained:
The rule in force with its full version history; the change record for every ceremony (both signatures, countdown, application, re-verification); and, for a deletion, the destruction attestation generated from the record.
C5 · Technical and behavioural requirements
C5.1 · Rules are plain-language, subject- and bindingness-classed [CANON]. Every rule reads as a plain sentence with its provenance; rules are sorted by subject and by bindingness; bindingness class is derived from the taxonomy, never hand-set, because a typed-in class would make the honesty claim itself unverifiable.
C5.2 · The change ceremony is mandatory and in the open [CANON]. Propose → counter-sign → visible countdown (cancellable) → apply → re-verify. Hard lines have no change path. The security service's own rules run the longest countdown and are flagged for watching when raised. A rail that cannot be demonstrated firing after a change is not in force — verification packs run at commissioning and again after every rule change.
C5.3 · Deletion is an authority act of the same shape [CANON]. Deletion names scope and reason, carries dual control, runs a cancellable waiting period, and ends in a destruction attestation generated from the record. Retention holds bar deletion absolutely; a hold is released only where it was created, never lifted from inside the system. Deletion events are recorded as entries, preserving append-only even as material leaves.
C5.4 · Every run records the rule version in force [CANON]. Without it, "was this term protected on a given date" has no answer — and that is exactly the question an audit asks. The rule version in force is stamped on the record.
C5.5 · Templates are adopted then governed like any rule [PROPOSED]. An adopted template (an external rulebook or framework) becomes a set of rules held to the same plain-language, bindingness-classed, ceremony-changed discipline as any authored rule. Adopting a template is a governed act; editing an adopted template runs the ceremony. Recommended so "templates adopted, edited" (the promise) inherits the full change discipline rather than being a bulk import that sits outside it.
C6 · What must be true for the promise to deliver as intended
No rule changes outside the ceremony. A change path that skips counter-signature or the countdown lets a rule change quietly or on one person's say-so — the exact thing the promise forbids.
Hard lines have no change path. If a hard line could be changed, "can never be switched off" is false.
The security service's own rules run the slowest, watched ceremony. If the Watch's rules could change quietly, the one exception of Part A would become a way to weaken the monitor unobserved.
Every run records the rule version in force. Without it, the audit question the whole record exists to answer has no answer.
Adopted templates inherit the ceremony (C5.5). A template imported outside the change discipline would be a set of rules nobody could change in the open.

Cross-Cutting Orchestration — The Shared Mechanisms
Document 16 of 16 · Canonical statement of requirements · The capstone
What this document holds: the mechanisms, doctrines, and composition rules that belong to no single promise because they are shared across many — stated once here, so the other fourteen documents reference them by name rather than re-deriving them. It also carries the two composite positioning claims (#5, #29) that are true of the whole platform rather than delivered by any one service.
Standalone statement of what must be true. This document is the one place the other fourteen point to; it points back to them by section where a mechanism's full local statement lives.
Source marking: [CANON] established in architectural canon · [RULED] owner ruling · [PROPOSED] synthesised to close a gap · [OPEN] genuine question, recommended default stated, not settled · [DETERMINED] a decision the owner instructed the author to make.
Why this document exists. Fourteen self-contained documents, each holding its own promise cluster, create one structural risk: a mechanism used by several of them either gets re-derived inconsistently in each, or — worse for a Markdown document set — dissolves entirely, because content that belongs to no single section tends to flatten out. This capstone is the deliberate home for exactly that content. Every item below is either a doctrine every document obeys, a mechanism several documents share, a composition rule binding two mechanisms together, or a whole-platform claim no single service owns.
§1 · THE COMPOSITE CLAIMS (#5, #29)
These two promises are not delivered by any single service. They are true — or false — as a consequence of many services each holding their own guarantee. They are stated here, in the one document whose job is the whole rather than a part, so they are neither given a false single owner nor lost.
§1.1 · Promise #5 — "All data in Akki is safe"
The claim. Every piece of data in the platform is safe — from leakage, from misuse, and from loss.
Why it has no single owner [RULED — composite claim]. "All data is safe" is true only if three independent guarantees all hold simultaneously, each owned by a different authority:
Nothing leaks — the custody crossing (Document 1): no data reaches an external model unmasked, unrecorded, or unpriced.
Nothing is misused — the Watch (Document 3): no actor, input, or action operates as something other than what it declared, undetected.
Nothing is lost or silently altered — the append-only record and authorised deletion (Documents 5, 14): nothing leaves the record except by a witnessed authority act, and corrections are new entries.
What must be true. #5 holds if and only if all three hold. It is not a feature to build; it is the standing consequence of Documents 1, 3, 5, and 14 each delivering. If any one fails, #5 is false regardless of the other two — which is why the platform states the three guarantees separately and never collapses them into a single "safe" checkbox that could read green while one underneath had failed.
§1.2 · Promise #29 — "Adaptive, superintelligent, yet contained"
The claim. The platform is powerful and adaptive, even superintelligent in capability, yet contained — because some of its engines remain deterministic.
Why it has no single owner [RULED — composite claim]. This is a positioning claim that resolves into properties owned across the platform:
Adaptive — the learning that is permitted: Targeta's yield layer, Mtafiti's inference overlay, Solva's reasoning faculty, the Watch's behavioural signature, the deflation flywheel (Documents 4, 9, 11, 13).
Superintelligent in reach — the breadth of what the platform can reason over, train, and orchestrate across a whole estate (Documents 4, 8, 13).
Yet contained — the deterministic governors and structural boundaries: Northena's determinism, the parameterless assertion boundary, the walled yield layer, the single crossing, and the rule that learning may detect but never decide (Documents 1, 4, 5, 14).
What must be true. #29 holds because the same system that is adaptive is contained by construction — the containment is not a limit bolted onto the adaptation, it is the architecture the adaptation runs inside. The one-line form: every learned component on the platform has the thing it could corrupt placed structurally out of its reach. #29 is true exactly as long as that remains so.
§2 · THE STRUCTURAL-IMPOSSIBILITY DOCTRINE
[CANON] Stated once; obeyed everywhere.
A prohibition enforced by review is eventually violated. A prohibition enforced by a function signature, a type, or a schema cannot be violated without the violation failing to compile or failing to persist. Every guarantee in this requirement set that can be made structural is made structural, not procedural.
The platform's sharpest instances, each specified in full in its home document:
the function computing what an answer may claim has no parameter through which a model's confidence can enter (Document 4);
the register that could hold identification biometrics has no field in which such a value could be placed (Document 1);
Targeta's yield layer receives a type it cannot turn into a non-permutation without raising (Document 13);
the single crossing is the only module that invokes the model client, enforced by negative-scan (Document 1);
Northena imports no learned component, enforced by import assertion (Document 5);
dual authority identity is unrepresentable in the task register, not policed (Documents 3, 14).
The doctrine as a requirement. When any document says a thing "cannot" happen, the correct implementation makes it structurally impossible — unrepresentable, non-compiling, or non-persisting — never merely forbidden and checked. A guarantee downgraded from structural to procedural during implementation is a defect against this doctrine, whatever else it does correctly.
Its honest companion — the unmeasured is never hidden [CANON]. The same honesty that makes impossibility structural makes gaps visible: anything not measured is recorded as unmeasured, never as zero, absent, or clean (Documents 9, 10). A blank that reads as clean makes the whole instrument dishonest. Structural impossibility and honest-about-the-unmeasured are the two halves of the platform's truthfulness: it cannot do what it says it cannot, and it never claims to know what it has not measured.
§3 · THE SHARED-CUSTODY RULING (retention lifecycles across documents)
[RULED] The reconciliation of the custody lifecycles the documents raise.
Three different objects carry three different lifespans, and they must never be collapsed into one another:
The per-request de-identification envelope (Document 1 §A5.3) — holds the token-to-value mapping; dies with the call, always. Retaining it would reconstitute exactly what the crossing protects.
The two files of the dual-file record (Document 2, Promise #4) — the question as asked and the extracted data before redaction; retained under their own custody class, distinct from the envelope.
The append-only record (Documents 5, 14) — the permanent audit account; nothing leaves it except by authorised deletion.
The ruling. These three lifecycles coexist without contradiction: the envelope dies with the call; the record lives indefinitely; the two files sit between — both retained in full, matching how they are actually used (a two-item context tied to one query, the same way an uploaded pair of context files stays attached to the request that referenced them). No document's invariant weakens another's, because the mechanism that keeps full retention from recreating the envelope's exposure is not reduction — it is governed read access: any consumption of the retained files after the original call, by anyone, for any reason, passes through the same masking discipline as a live crossing (Document 1 §A5.2). Full retention is a write-time decision; full governance is a permanent, read-time one. Together they are what let both files stay whole without recreating the standing exposure the crossing exists to prevent.
[RULED] Both files retained in full. Read-time re-masking on every subsequent access. Recorded here as the single shared-custody decision; Documents 1 and 2 both resolve to this.
§4 · THE DECIDE-ONCE-AT-THE-BOUNDARY PATTERN
[CANON] One pattern, three instances — named here so it is understood as one.
A determination that could disagree with itself if re-derived downstream is made exactly once, at a boundary, against a frozen input, and honoured everywhere after — because a second derivation could disagree with the first, and the consumer would have been told one thing and given another. The three instances:
The warm/fresh fork (Documents 5 §7, 8 §B5.1) — served-from-memory vs. fresh-extraction, decided once at Northena's Gate by a membership test against a frozen set plus a convergence check, never re-derived.
The objective freeze (Documents 5 §6.2, 14 §B5.3) — what a run is for, frozen at Admit; a changed intent is a new admission, never an edit of a live one.
The task-identity mapping (Documents 3, 14, §5 below) — which authority a task acts under, mapped once before execution; an unmapped task class does not run.
The pattern as a requirement [RULED — recognised as one discipline, three implementations]. Each instance is specified in full in its home document. Recognising them as one pattern matters for implementation: the same discipline (decide once, freeze the input, forbid re-derivation) governs each, and a fourth boundary decision added later follows the same shape rather than inventing a new one.
[RULED] Three separate implementations of one shared discipline, not one shared code primitive — the inputs and frozen sets genuinely differ, and a shared primitive would risk coupling three boundaries that should be able to change independently. The discipline is shared; the code is not. This item has no product, security, or business consequence either way — it is a build detail the engineering team can apply directly from this ruling without further owner involvement.
§5 · THE ONE-RECORD LAW AND THE RECORD MATRIX
[CANON] The single most-referenced shared mechanism (5 documents).
There is exactly one record, and nothing maintains a second. What the law forbids is a second, competing account of the same events; what it requires is one authoritative account per record class, with a single writing authority and named readers. This is the Mkondo charter (Document 14's Part A basis), and it is the home of every "who writes / who reads / who acts" question the other documents raise.
The three registers, stated once:
The record matrix — every record class, its single writer, its named readers. A second writer to any class is a defect on sight; a reader not listed does not read.
The task-identity register — every task class maps to exactly one authority's identity and scope before it executes; an unmapped task class does not run; no credential scope spans two authorities.
The handoff contracts — every crossing between services or authorities states what crosses, in what shape, under whose identity, with what receipt; an unregistered handoff is a finding.
What the documents delegate here. The Ledger's ownership (Document 5), the Watch's own record classes (Documents 3, 14), the dual-file record's account (Document 2), the disclosure record (Document 6), and the cost/trace records (Documents 4, 11) are all classes in this one matrix. Where any document asked "who owns this record," the answer is: one writer per class, named here, never answered from memory and never differently in two places.
§6 · DECLARED-VS-DEMONSTRATED DIVERGENCE — TWO MECHANISMS, NEVER ONE
[RULED] Resolved on the platform's own authority-separation doctrine — this turned out to be the sharper of the two arguments available.
The platform reconciles what an actor declared against what its conduct demonstrates in two places:
The Watch (Document 3 §B5.4) — reconciles an actor's realised behaviour against its frozen mandate, across the actor's whole presence on the estate; divergence is a security emission that can drive containment.
Solva (Document 4 §B) — reconciles an asker's demonstrated conduct against its declared frame, within a single reasoning session; divergence is a reasoning-discipline signal that constrains the synthesis and is emitted for the Watch to read.
A third, smaller instance was raised in Document 6: a processing step requesting far more context than its declared task warrants is the same shape of signal (disclosure-breadth divergence) — this stays its own emission, not folded into either of the two below.
The ruling, and the real reason. These are two mechanisms, never one — not primarily because they differ in scope, clock, and consequence (true, but not the load-bearing reason), but because the Watch and Solva are different authorities, and no mechanism is permitted to span both. Solva is one of the six engines that make up the governing authority (Document 1 §7.1). The Watch is the second, peer authority, standing beside the six, subordinate to none (Document 3, Document 1 §7.1). This is the identical rule that makes dual task-identity unrepresentable in Mkondo's register (Document 15 §5, "no credential scope exists that spans both authorities") — a mechanism shared between the Watch's divergence-tracking and Solva's would be exactly that kind of cross-authority span, just implemented as shared logic instead of a shared credential. If the two authorities shared this mechanism, a compromise or change on one side could quietly alter the other's judgment — the same failure the credential-separation rule exists to prevent, recreated one layer up.
What is shared, and what is not. The vocabulary and record shape — "declared class, demonstrated class, divergence tracked over time" — is common to both, so an auditor reads divergence the same way wherever it appears. The mechanism itself is not shared: the Watch's tracking and Solva's tracking are built, run, and owned separately, exactly as the platform's peer-authority architecture already requires everywhere else. The Solva→Watch emission (already specified) is the seam that keeps the two coherent without merging them.
§7 · THE MARK-TWICE-AT-SEAMS DISCIPLINE
[CANON] A discipline that must be applied wherever a marked artifact crosses a seam.
A load-bearing marker enforced only at the point it is written can be dropped by a downstream step that never knew it was load-bearing. Therefore an advisory or governance marker is enforced twice: at write time by the producing service, and again at the point of consumption (render, re-association, or hand-off) by the consuming step.
Its instances across the documents:
Advisory outputs are marked at write time and again at render time (Document 4 §A5.1).
The dual-file re-association seam re-checks each file's markers rather than trusting the original per-file assessment (Document 2 §B5.3).
A handoff contract carries provenance marks that the receiving side reads, not assumes (Documents 14, §5 above).
The discipline as a requirement [PROPOSED — that it is applied uniformly]. Wherever this requirement set specifies a marker that governs behaviour, that marker is re-verified at every seam it crosses, never trusted from its origin alone. A seam that trusts an upstream marker blindly is where the marker silently dies — and the re-association seam of Promise #4 is the sharpest example, because two independently-cleared files meeting there is exactly where a dropped "still-contains-something" marker would let material through.
§8 · THE CLOSED REFUSAL TAXONOMY
[CANON] Referenced as shared platform-wide in Document 1; given its proper home here.
A refusal is a defined, first-class outcome across the platform, never an error, a timeout, or a silent degradation. It is drawn from a closed vocabulary — no ad-hoc refusal path exists anywhere, enforced structurally rather than by review.
Its instances across the documents:
The custody crossing refuses a request that cannot be safely served, with a plain reason and the options actually available (Document 1).
Solva's assertion boundary refuses a conclusion below the objective's defensibility floor, visibly, with the three actions the requester can take — never silently downgraded and served (Document 4).
Late refusal is a first-class terminal state: a run admitted and later found inadmissible has not failed, it has been governed, and is rendered as a governed refusal (Document 8, Document 12).
Hard-line rules have no change path and refuse absolutely; a held item under a retention hold produces an absolute refusal naming the hold instrument (Document 15).
Refusals and drops are recorded exactly as successes are — a system that drops what it declined cannot show what it declined (Document 5).
What must be true. The vocabulary of refusal reasons is closed and consistent wherever it is rendered — a caller sees the same shape of refusal whether it originated at the crossing, at the assertion boundary, or at a governance hold. No document, service, or surface introduces a refusal shape outside this closed set; a new refusal reason is a governed addition to the taxonomy, never a local invention.
§9 · THE SERVING-DISCIPLINE UNIFICATION (LC-3 = progressive disclosure = on-demand fetch)
[RULED] Three names, one mechanism — the most important composition in the set.
Three documents describe what is, and must be built as, one serving discipline:
Progressive disclosure (Document 6, #17) — minimal-sufficient context served to any processing agent first, expanded on demand, protecting agents from confusion before a human asks.
On-demand fetch / the traffic controller (Document 8, #21) — fetching the specific thing needed without re-running the estate.
Retrieval narrowing / LC-3 (Document 11, #9) — the cost-facing name for sending a model only what it needs.
The ruling. These are not three mechanisms. They are one serving discipline seen from three angles: focus (Document 6), specificity (Document 8), and cost (Document 11). It is built once and referenced by all three. A consumer served by one path is served by all, because there is one path.
What must be true. If built as three, a consumer narrowed for cost might not be narrowed for focus, an agent protected from confusion might still pull the whole estate for a specific fetch, and three implementations drift apart. The requirement is one implemented serving discipline with three documented consequences.
§10 · THE LC-1-VS-WARM-SERVE COMPOSITION (distinct, composed, not merged)
[RULED] Two mechanisms that look alike and must not be merged.
Warm-serving (Document 8) is estate-level: has this data been mined into qualified intelligence already?
LC-1, the response cache (Document 11) is query-level: has this exact governed question been answered already?
The ruling. They compose — an answer served warm from qualified intelligence can also be response-cached on an identical repeat, saving even the warm composition — but they are distinct mechanisms answering distinct questions, and building them as one would break both: a query-cache that thought it was an estate-memory would serve stale answers when the estate changed; an estate-memory that thought it was a query-cache would re-mine on every rephrasing. LC-1 invalidates on estate freshness (Document 11 §B1.4) — the seam that keeps the two coherent: when the estate changes, the query cache built on it is invalidated, so the faster mechanism never outlives the truth the slower one holds.
§11 · THE THINGS THAT MUST STAY IN THEIR AXIS
[CANON] The separation-of-authority summary, collected.
Several documents each state that their engine does one thing and never another. Collected here, because the set of separations is itself a platform property: no single point may admit, bound, and assert a run by the same logic, or there is no separate check left to catch it.
Northena governs direction only — never reasons, never extracts (Document 5).
SyniSense governs the boundary only — the crossing, not the reasoning (Document 1).
Solva governs depth only — reasons, never extracts, never enforces (Document 4).
Mtafiti measures only — never extracts, targets, or governs (Document 9).
Targeta targets only — learns yield, never touches the floor or eligibility (Document 13).
CIF governs ideation/judgement across the others' walls — peer, subordinate to none (referenced where CIF's promises are held).
The Watch is the security authority — detects and contains, never governs a governance verdict; and it is contained by its own instance of the disciplines, not the shared one (Documents 3, 14 §A5).
Mkondo governs structure only — admits nothing, judges nothing, detects nothing (Documents 14, §5 above).
The requirement. A change that lets any one of these perform another's function is a defect against the platform's core safety property, however locally convenient. The axes never collapse.
§12 · WHAT THE CAPSTONE COMES DOWN TO
The fifteen documents each hold a promise and its service. This document holds what binds them: two claims true of the whole and no part (#5, #29); one doctrine every guarantee obeys (structural impossibility, with its honest companion); one custody reconciliation across three lifecycles; one boundary-decision pattern in three places; one record law with its three registers; the ruling that closed the set's one genuinely open architectural question (declared-vs-demonstrated: two mechanisms, never one — different authorities, never a shared span); one mark-twice discipline; the closed refusal taxonomy; two compositions that must not be merged (the serving discipline; LC-1 vs. warm-serve); and one collected separation-of-authority that is itself a platform property. Where any of the fifteen names one of these, it names it here — once, so it is never re-derived differently in two places, and never flattened out of a document that had no room for it.
