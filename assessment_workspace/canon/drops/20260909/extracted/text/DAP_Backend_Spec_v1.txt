RMS Data Access Portal
dap_backend_spec_v1 — Draft for owner ratification
1   Purpose & Scope
DAP is how an estate owner monetises a governed estate without surrendering control of it, and how an external party — an AI lab, a research institution, a commercial buyer — acquires what it needs from that estate under terms the owner sets and the platform enforces.
Six operations are in scope: browse an estate's composition; connect an external workspace by API key; extract processed intelligence; fine-tune a model on estate data; develop skills against estate data; and download raw data. These are not six variants of one action. They are six distinct crossings with six different governance profiles, and §4 states each one separately because collapsing them would defeat every guarantee the platform makes.
Two sides are in scope: the external-party side (a party acquiring) and the estate-owner side (an owner configuring what is available, on what terms, and approving what requires approval). The external-party side is built first; the owner side reuses Manage Rules and Spend Requests where they already exist.
2   Definitions
3   The External-Party Principal
The governed triad (Document 0, Attribute 3) has no class for a party outside the estate owner's own organisation. DAP adds one, because every requirement below is stated over it.
DAP-1  An external party is admitted as an organisation-level principal carrying: legal identity; a declared governance posture; the terms it is admitted under; its trust domain identifier; and an admission-time mandate stating what it came to do. Individuals and agents act under it, never beside it.
DAP-2  Every request carries a party principal — a named individual or a registered agent — with its own mandate, mark state, and behavioural signature under the Watch. An agent's mandate is separate from its operator's and is admitted separately.  [Doc 3 #7]
DAP-3  Connecting an external agent admits an autonomous non-human principal. It MUST be admitted as its own principal under DAP-2, MUST carry a stated scope of autonomy, and is subject to the Watch's machine-speed attack shape from its first request.
DAP-4  Slow-play extraction — a sequence of individually-permitted requests whose aggregate would reconstruct material the party is not entitled to — MUST be detected as a distinct attack shape against the party's cumulative extraction history, not evaluated per request.  [S-2]
DAP-4 is the reason cumulative extraction history is a first-class record (DAP-24) rather than a reporting convenience. Every individual request in a slow-play sequence is legitimate; only the aggregate is not.
DAP-5  DAP is a trust-domain boundary. The external party's domain and the estate's domain share no identifier resolution, no store, and no credential. A unit identifier meaningful inside the estate MUST NOT resolve to anything in the party's domain except through an operation §4 permits.  [Attribute 12]
4   The Six Crossing Profiles
Each operation is governed differently. This table is the spine of the specification: every requirement in §5 and §6 implements a row of it, and no operation may be added to DAP without a row stating its profile.
What the profiles mean in practice
DAP-6  Browse MUST return aggregate composition only — volume, type, language, period, counts per type — and MUST NOT return, sample, or preview unit contents. Browsing an estate is not a crossing and MUST NOT be recorded as one.  [Mtafiti]
DAP-7  A connected workspace's queries execute inside the estate's perimeter. The party's query enters; de-identified results leave. Estate data MUST NOT leave under this operation, and no code path may make it do so.  [Promise #10, the pull seam]
DAP-8  Processed intelligence egresses derived output only. The source units read to produce it MUST be recorded in the receipt and MUST NOT be reconstructable from the output. Where an objective cannot be served without exposing source, the operation is refused, not degraded.  [Attribute 7]
DAP-9  Fine-tuning is compute-to-data only. The party's base model is admitted to estate compute; estate data MUST NOT be transferred to compute outside the estate's perimeter under any configuration. There is no permitted inverse of this operation.  [Owner ruling, 5 Sep 2026]
DAP-10  Trained weights and skill files leave as artefact egress: through the model registry, with a rights posture stamped, a hash recorded, and the terms in force cited. Weights derived from controlled units carry the derivation on the registry entry.  [#24, #30]
DAP-11  Raw download is the outer-gate crossing of Promise #25, not the ordinary chokepoint crossing. Disclosure depth is bounded by rights and custody; the redaction pass runs as a gate, not a prompt; and the operation writes an evidence record. Every unit released is individually recorded.  [#25]
DAP-9 states a prohibition with no exception clause because the compute-to-data direction is the platform's sovereignty guarantee. A configuration flag permitting the inverse would make the guarantee a preference.
5   Back-End Services
Admission & keys
DAP-12  Admission of an external party is a governed act producing: the organisation principal (DAP-1), its trust domain, its terms, and its mandate. Admission MUST be recorded and MUST require the estate owner's named authority. A party is never self-admitting.
DAP-13  API keys are issued per party principal, never per organisation, and carry the principal's mandate. A key MUST be revocable in a single act with immediate effect, MUST expire on a stated lifetime (§10 R-4), and MUST NOT be transferable between principals. Key material is never written to any record, including receipts.  [A5.7]
Approval
DAP-14  A request containing at least one controlled unit MUST route to the estate owner's named approving authority before execution, carrying: the requesting principal, the objective, the units or scope in question, why each is controlled, and the terms that would apply. The request does not execute while pending.
DAP-15  Approval decisions are recorded with the deciding authority's identity, the decision, and its stated reason. A refusal states why. Neither approval nor refusal may be inferred from silence — an unanswered request expires unexecuted rather than proceeding by default.  [fail-closed]
DAP-16  Fine-tune and skill-development requests always require approval, controlled or not, because both produce artefact egress (DAP-10). Raw download always requires approval where the request is controlled; the estate owner configures whether uncontrolled raw download requires it (DAP-22).
DAP-17  An estate owner MAY grant a standing approval for a named party, a named scope, and a stated window — replacing per-request approval within it. A standing approval is itself an approved act under DAP-14's discipline, is revocable in one act, and MUST NOT cover controlled units unless it names them explicitly.
Extraction
DAP-18  Every extraction is a commissioned job with a frozen objective. Objective freeze applies at commission: after commission, no path — including the portal's own assistance chat — may widen the job's scope. A wider scope is a new job.  [#32, Northena]
DAP-19  Every job routes to the engine that governs its operation per §4 and MUST NOT execute through any other path. DAP holds no extraction logic of its own; it composes.
DAP-20  The portal's assistance chat MAY help a party shape an objective, identify relevant scope, or understand terms, before commission. It MUST NOT approve, widen scope after commission, or decide any gate. It proposes; the gates decide.  [Attribute 10]
Metering & receipts
DAP-21  Every operation in §4 emits a metered event carrying: operation type; what was accessed (unit identifiers, or the aggregate scope where units are not individually released); quantity in the unit natural to the operation — audio-hours, tokens, compute-seconds, units, queries; the terms identifier in force; the party principal; and the timestamp from the platform's reliable time source. Metered events are written to the Ledger before the operation's result is returned.  [AU-3]
DAP-22  Terms attach at three levels, resolved most-specific-first: per party agreement, then per custody class, then estate default. Every receipt cites the terms identifier actually applied, not the level it came from.
DAP-23  Spend against a party's ceiling is evaluated before execution. An operation that would exceed the ceiling routes to a person as a spend request rather than failing silently or executing over-budget.  [#11]
DAP-24  Cumulative extraction history per party is a first-class, queryable record — what has been taken, in what quantity, under which terms, over what period — retained for the party's full admission, and readable by the estate owner at any time.  [supports DAP-4]
DAP-21 through DAP-24 are the pricing rails, not a pricing model. Whatever tariff is later ruled — per audio-hour, per token, per compute-hour, subscription, or revenue share — every event required to compute it is already on the Ledger, attributed and timestamped. §10 R-6 carries the tariff as a named dependency.
Estate-owner configuration
DAP-25  An estate owner configures, per custody class and per operation, whether that operation is available at all, available on approval, or available under standing terms. The default for every combination is unavailable — availability is granted, never assumed.  [fail-closed]
DAP-26  An estate owner sets default terms per custody class, per-party overrides, and per-party ceilings. Changes take effect through the Rules Service ceremony for the estate's own rules and MUST NOT retroactively alter the terms cited on receipts already issued.
DAP-27  An estate owner MUST be able to suspend a party — halting every in-flight and future operation for that party in one act — and to revoke its admission. Suspension is immediate; in-flight jobs stop and are recorded as stopped, not silently completed.  [Doc 3 A5.3 — stop, never delete]
6   Failure Posture
DAP-28  Every gate in DAP fails closed. An approval service that cannot be reached, a metering write that cannot complete, a terms resolution that returns nothing, a ceiling check that errors — each refuses the operation. No path returns estate data, an artefact, or a query result when a governing check could not complete.  [FPT_FLS.1]
DAP-29  A metered event that cannot be written to the Ledger refuses the operation it meters. An unrecorded extraction is not permitted to have happened.  [I-4 pattern]
DAP-30  Browse degrades rather than refuses: where the census is stale or partially unavailable, DAP returns what it has, marked as stale with its as-of time, and never silently presents an incomplete estate as complete.  [honesty grammar]

11   Standing
DAP is the first instance of a generic Data & Intelligence Acquisition Platform. Nothing in §1–§9 is specific to RMS except the named estate; a second estate owner adopting this platform inherits every requirement here and configures §5's owner-side settings for its own estate.
The front-end specification — the three-panel layout, navigation, per-screen content, and the assistance chat's interaction design — depends on this document and follows it. Where the two conflict, this document governs: the front end may not offer an operation §4 does not profile.
