Executive interpretation
The strongest defensible quantitative statement is narrower than an estate-wide sales claim: an
authorised staged subset contains 3,958 readable-header audio files, totalling 529.34 file-
duration hours and 36.52 decimal GB. A separately assembled, labelled transcript corpus
contains 157,513 natural units and 27,329,904 characters from 4,521 source files. Neither
statement certifies the intersection of non-English content, non-music speech, acceptable quality
and external-use rights. [R07]

This is not a finding that the estate has no suitable material. It is a finding that candidate
inventory and eligible corpus have not yet been reconciled. A global AI lab should receive both
the positive inventory and the disqualifying uncertainties, not a headline with its qualifiers hidden
in an appendix. [R07]

There is a credible near-term route to value: owner-controlled search and cited context, editorial
archive workflows, specialised evaluation and carefully scoped local-language tooling. Open-
source software can be released independently of raw data. Corpus licensing, broad model
releases and voice products require additional evidence and permission. The options later in this
report are advisory propositions, not booked revenue or authorisation to use the data. [R07]


How to read this report
Measured means a retained census, listing, header or worker record reports an observation
within a stated scope. It does not mean every observation is independently certified. Derived
means transparent arithmetic over those observations. Estimated means sampling, inference or
an indicative tariff is involved. Proposed means a recommended design, commercial option or
future work item. Unknown means the required evidence is absent; it never means zero. [R07]

Counts refer to the named snapshot, not necessarily the live estate today. The relational census
was measured on 29 September 2026; listings have their own timestamps. The reporting
evidence cut is 1 October 2026 at 19:12 UTC. Audio hours sum file headers; overlapping,
replayed or copied recordings may represent the same underlying speech. GB and TB in this
report are decimal; GiB and TiB are binary. Character counts, source bytes, training units and
tokenizer-specific tokens are deliberately kept separate. [R07]

Duplication and storage rationalisation belong in Part I. Part II answers corpus quantity, content
qualification and task quality; it uses the already assembled natural-unit counts and refers back
to Part I for uniqueness controls rather than conflating file counts with independent examples.
No destructive data operation, external release or new model training was commissioned to
produce this report. [R07]

Quality and privacy signals are material, but their denominators
differ
The headline weighted signals are 1.537% exact duplicate rows, 3.584% normalised duplicates,
22.957% degraded rows, 81.695% null-bearing rows, 71.833% personal-data-marker rows and
85.313% near-duplicate text. These measures concern different measured or sampled
populations. Personal-data checking covered 1,011,330 rows. The report does not establish a
common independent sampling design; the percentages cannot be multiplied by the entire
estimated row inventory as if they were population parameters. [R01]

Degradation-kind signals include truncation at 21.91%, invalid format at 6.26%, placeholder
content at 1.26% and whitespace anomalies at 0.94% of the relevant weighted measurement. A
row may exhibit several kinds. The census also identifies 983 near-empty columns among 7,563
measured columns. These are operational leads, not proof of semantic corruption in every
affected value. [R01]

The condition-byte indicators overlap. In particular, null-bearing, degraded and stale byte
estimates are not disjoint partitions; an apparent zero “clean bytes” residual is not proof that no
useful material exists. Recompute an exclusive condition taxonomy only after defining what
completeness and fitness mean for each data product. Personal-data markers trigger
minimisation and review, not an automatic blanket prohibition or legal conclusion. [R01]


Duplication has several meanings and several confidence levels
Within-table exact matching excludes the primary key. Normalised matching applies additional
normalisation; near-duplicate text uses text sketches rather than semantic embeddings. The
large near-duplicate-text signal may include repeated templates, boilerplate or log formats. It
does not mean that the same fraction of the whole estate is safely deletable. Similarly, same-
schema table overlap is a sketch-based estimate, not an authoritative row-by-row equivalence
proof. [R01/R04]

The relational report identifies 71 cross-table overlap pairs, approximately 1.302 GB of within-
table duplicate-byte candidates and 0.0566 GB of cross-table duplicate-byte candidates.
Separately, the transcript assembly removed 2,525 repeated units before its natural-unit
inventory was emitted. Those are different operations on different populations; neither should be
subtracted again from the counts in Part II. [R01/R04]

Freshness and growth need field-level interpretation
The census flags 290 tables with a latest selected timestamp older than a year, representing
approximately 58.132 GB. It also records future-dated or extreme historical values in some
timestamp columns. Examples include dates beyond the reporting cut and sentinel-like ancient
values. These can be legitimate event dates, entry errors, schema defaults or the wrong field for
measuring modification age. [R01]

A selected latest timestamp is not automatically a last-write time. Validate each field’s
semantics, time zone, clock behaviour and update path. Repeated identical byte counts are not
proof of zero business activity; catalogue size estimates can remain flat while rows change.
Likewise, small row deltas between snapshots may reflect estimates or asynchronous
measurement rather than a reliable growth rate. [R01]

Define a freshness contract for each product: publication time for editorial context, last confirmed
validity for reference data, transaction settlement time for operational analytics and last content
observation for media. Recompute staleness under that contract. This is more actionable than
applying a single age threshold to the entire estate. [R01]

Capture properties are measurable; quality grades are not yet
established
The measured sample-rate distribution is 3,126 files at 44.1 kHz; 162 at 48 kHz; 49 at 22.05
kHz; 597 at 11.025 kHz; 22 at 16 kHz; and 2 at 32 kHz. These counts sum to the staged
inventory. No unreadable headers were reported. This supports header accessibility and
capture-format accounting only; it does not establish successful full decoding, low noise,
intelligibility or correct transcripts. [R03]

A low sampling rate limits available bandwidth, but a high sampling rate cannot restore
information already lost upstream. Container size also depends on encoding, bit depth,
channels, metadata and compression—not just duration or quality. Do not assign “studio”,
“telephone” or “broadcast-clean” grades from sample rate or file extension. [R03]

A proper audio-quality distribution needs full-decode success, effective bandwidth, clipped-
sample share, silence and speech-active duration, loudness variability, channel consistency,
noise and reverberation measures, overlapping speech, intelligibility and task-relevant human
evaluation. Thresholds should be chosen for the intended task and validated on native-speaker

Define non-English and non-music at the segment level
Use mutually understandable content categories: local-language speech without music; English-
only speech; code-switched speech; music-only; speech with music underneath; other sound;
silence; and unresolved. A file can contain several categories, so file-level inclusion is too coarse
for a strict speech inventory. Speech over a music bed is not automatically “non-music”; classify
it separately unless the recipient explicitly accepts it. [R07]

Define the target before counting. Strict non-English speech can exclude any English-bearing
segment; a local-language-dominant product can instead retain code-switched segments with
token- or time-based proportions and explicit flags. These are different products and cannot
share an unqualified headline. Lyric text, songs, station jingles and promotional beds also need
treatment when qualifying transcripts. [R07]

Automated speech/music and language scores can prioritise review, but their errors must be
measured on independent native-speaker annotations. Unknown labels stay unknown. A bucket
named for music is a useful exclusion lead, not proof that music is absent elsewhere. Likewise a
local-language folder can contain English quotations, presenters introducing songs or mixed
programmes. [R07]


Text quantity is real, but units, characters and tokens are different
The assembled corpus is dominated by Kalenjin and Wanga-labelled text units, followed by
Dholuo, Ekegusii, Kiswahili, Kikuyu and Kikamba. Unit counts reflect the assembly’s
segmentation rule; they are not word counts, unique speakers or independent documents. A
short sentence and a longer utterance can both count as one unit, so a row-count comparison
alone does not describe training mass. [R04/R05]

The character count is an observed assembly measure. It is not UTF-8 bytes, and a generic
characters-per-token divisor would not produce a defensible multilingual token inventory.
Tokenisation varies by model vocabulary and language. Report tokenizer name/version,
normalisation, special tokens, truncation and whether counts are unique input tokens or
repeated training exposure. [R04/R05]

Transcript quality and aligned speech hours are still open
No independent word-error-rate or character-error-rate distribution has been established for the
transcript corpus in the reviewed evidence. Folder language, successful assembly and high
language-identification performance cannot substitute for transcript fidelity. Punctuation,
diacritics, named entities, local orthography, borrowed words and code-switches deserve
separate treatment. [R03/R04/R07]

Paired speech–text value requires a verified join: canonical audio ID and hash, segment

Compute eligible hours with an auditable segment ledger
For a strict speech corpus, eligible hours equal the sum of segment duration in seconds divided
by 3,600, restricted to segments whose language policy, music policy, quality policy, rights basis
and intended use all pass. Unresolved gates remain unresolved; they do not become pass
values or count as zero-duration assets. Report known-included, known-excluded and
unresolved duration separately. [R07]

The asset ledger should include source system, canonical ID, cryptographic hash, recording
timestamp, duration, channel and codec properties, source rights, segment labels, confidence,
annotation origin, quality indicators and all exclusions. Retain the versioned policy applied.
When only a sample is annotated, distinguish the observed qualifying hours from an estimated
population yield. [R07]

Use duration-weighted, stratified estimation with the actual inclusion probabilities. Cluster by
programme/session or speaker where appropriate; an asset-count sample can be biased toward
short files. Confidence intervals must reflect the sampling design and classifier validation, not



PP-RMS-ESTATE-20261001 · Owner confidential · controlled circulation · page 12
just a binomial formula over correlated segments. No estate-wide confidence interval is claimed
in this report because the necessary design and labels are not yet present. [R07]


A sampling illustration is not a substitute for the actual design
As a planning illustration only, a simple random sample of independent observations needs
about 385 observations to estimate a proportion within five percentage points at approximately
95% confidence under the worst-case proportion of one half. This follows the normal-
approximation expression n = z² p(1−p) / e² with z = 1.96. It is not an adopted requirement or a
measured sample size for this estate. [R07]

Broadcast segments are clustered and languages are imbalanced, so effective sample size can
be far smaller than the raw segment count. Rare languages, music beds, code-switching and
failure cases may require deliberate oversampling. Weight results back to the population design
and preserve separate per-stratum uncertainty. Confirm exact or bootstrap intervals where
assumptions warrant them. [R07]

Even a sample with no detected contamination does not prove zero contamination in the
population. The recipient and data owner must agree tolerances and escalation rules. The
operational aim is an honest, reproducible statement of expected quality and uncertainty, not a
convenient green badge. [R07]


What a global AI lab should receive
Provide a versioned data card and machine-readable manifest with source provenance;
permitted and prohibited uses; recording and publication dates; language/dialect and code-
switch distributions; hours and bytes by segment category; encoding and sampling properties;
verified quality metrics and uncertainty; annotation and transcript provenance; rights and privacy
exclusions; and known representational limitations. The data card should identify who is
responsible for corrections and withdrawals. [R07/R08]

The acceptance pack should include sample selection and adjudication protocols, inter-
annotator disagreement, task-specific validation, complete failure/exclusion counts, canonical
split IDs, contamination controls, exact tokenizer counts where relevant, reproducible
code/configuration hashes, and a documented way to reproduce the reported figures. It should
distinguish source-level consent from processing agreements, model-training permissions,

The wider portfolio extends beyond corpus sales and context APIs
The remaining options strengthen the portfolio without requiring an unqualified wholesale data
release: private editorial search, terminology and knowledge resources, translation/localisation
support, bounded adapters, aggregate media intelligence and native-speaker data-quality
services. Several reinforce one another; annotation improves the benchmark, the benchmark
strengthens the licence offer, and a curated lexicon improves retrieval and localisation. [R07]

Sequence by evidence readiness and reversibility, not by the most ambitious marketing label. An
internal archive-search pilot or an open tooling release is generally easier to scope than external
raw-corpus licensing or broad weight release, but neither is automatically ready: rights, accurate
claims and operational support still matter. Use paid or institutionally funded pilots to test
demand and delivery cost. [R07]

Avoid starting a voice-cloning product from existing broadcast voices. If synthetic voice is ever
considered, use a separately commissioned, explicitly consented voice-actor programme and a
distinct risk review. Similarly, synthetic training augmentation should be a labelled, auditable

data inventory. [R07]

An open ecosystem and a commercial service can coexist
Release boundaries should be explicit. Code can use an open-source licence while restricted
data stays private. A dataset can permit research without permitting redistribution; that is not the
same as open data. Open weights still depend on base-model terms, training-data rights and
downstream restrictions. A hosted API can be public to access without its code, weights or
underlying data being open. [R07/R08]

A practical public-interest track is to publish schemas, data-card templates, language/dialect
documentation, safe examples, evaluation methods and tooling that local teams can reproduce.
Create mechanisms for native-speaker corrections and community governance. When suitable
content is cleared, release a deliberately bounded subset with provenance and a
contact/withdrawal process. [R07/R08]

Fund maintenance rather than only the initial launch: documentation, security updates, issue
triage, evaluation refresh, contributor support and hosting all require ownership. Measure useful
independent reuse, not just repository stars or download counts. Commercial support,
consortium funding and hosted services can help sustainability, but their economics should be
validated rather than assumed. [R07/R08]
