RMS estate and language corpus: what is defensible
Owner briefing on estate condition, the corpus claim and responsible options
Royal Media Services · Owner briefing · Owner confidential · controlled circulation
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The estate is large but only partly measured; a narrow language claim is defensible now
SITUATION
The retained database catalogue holds 377199771648 bytes across 1006 tables, with an estimated 259273874 rows. Separate inventories list 143284816848890 bytes in 22975591 objects at OBS, and 47990537059397 bytes in 1456787 files at NAS. A staged audio subset and a separately assembled text corpus have also been measured.
COMPLICATION
Only 638 table entries were measured, and the census observed 5881277 rows against an estimate of 259273874. The file inventories record custody, not language, music or rights, and they cannot be added together. No final quality receipt exists, so no estate-wide hours or token claim is yet possible.
QUESTION
What can the owner responsibly claim and commercialise today, and what must be done first to widen that claim?
ANSWER
Claim only what is measured: 529.3 hours of staged audio across 3958 files, and 157513 natural text units from 4521 source files. Language identification reached 97.2% conditional macro accuracy on 2800 units, but it is weaker for Kikamba and the Wanga / Luhya label. Complete the coverage ledger and rights review before any licensing, release or revenue statement.
