**How to read these numbers:** I assessed five essential delivery blocks per promise, distinguishing foundations, connected functionality, real working slices and independently verified complete outcomes. These are rounded engineering judgments, best read within approximately ten percentage points—not measured probabilities. **“Shield 40% delivered” does not mean “40% secure.”** A critical protection gap cannot be averaged away.

The earlier M1–M10 estimates measured narrower workstreams. A mostly built reporting engine, for example, does not establish an excellent, affordable, governed delivery service across all advertised tasks.

## 1. Governed model training — **~50%**

### What is genuinely present
- Real text and specialist audio training, with recorded results, rejected candidates and scoped admissions.
- Training plans, execution controls, evaluation records and model-registry foundations.
- Working private uploads with checksums, quotas, access controls and rights metadata.

### What prevents the full promise
**Uploading data is not yet equivalent to training on it.** All four current uploaded-file assessment records report `held_before_retraining` and `training_started=false`.

The general training backend remains unimplemented. Its fixture backend is **MOCKED/SYNTHETIC**; the specialist training pipelines are real. The 13-language task also needs dataset, worker and reporting changes—not just permission to launch.

The missing complete journey is:

**Select estate/uploaded data → qualify it → select an admitted recipe/model → train → independently evaluate → admit within scope → serve or deliver.**

**Canon qualification:** “Any output dimension” was prescribed, but it does not mean every arbitrary architecture is automatically trainable. Canon favours bounded adaptation of registered, licensed bases. We need an honest supported-model/task matrix—not an unrestricted claim about proprietary weights, every modality or foundation-model pretraining.

## 2. Context-as-a-Service foundation — **~45%**

### What is genuinely present
Estate intelligence, source-linked reporting, identity and entitlement foundations, governed context contracts, and specific extraction/delivery capabilities.

### What prevents the full promise
The foundation must support a repeatable external customer journey:

**Admit customer/application → grant scoped access → commission work → provide governed context/results → meter consumption → bill → revoke access when terms change.**

That complete commercial journey is not established. The current operator’s October billing view resolves successfully but contains no billable lines; this is not evidence of a working nonempty commercial cycle.

Marketing, content mining, media planning and campaign strategy are credible applications of the architecture. **They are not delivered products merely because the underlying data and LLM are available.** Each needs a defined input, output, acceptance standard and permitted operating scope.

**“Without moving data” needs precise wording:** raw estate data stays under estate custody; authorized compute accesses it there; permitted derived or de-identified context may leave through the appropriate gate. That is different from promising that no bytes ever move.

## 3. Right agent/model, excellent output, lower cost — **~50%**

### What is genuinely present
- Real Word, PowerPoint and Excel production, with revisions, checks and downloads.
- Multi-turn chat and context handling.
- Assigned planner, writer and reviewer roles, including different model families.

### What prevents the full promise
**Configured role assignments are not yet an evidence-driven “best model for this task” selector.** The implementation explicitly states that no independent head-to-head comparison on owner data was performed for those assignments.

Canon already requires the **lowest-cost model that meets the task’s standard**, based on measured performance. “Best” should therefore mean best fit within quality, confidentiality, latency and budget constraints—not automatically the newest or largest model.

The stronger output and cost claims are also unsubstantiated:
- The existing 200-task evaluation passed 161 checks, but it is not a factual-quality or creative-quality certification.
- The Cost Ledger reports partial costing, one model-rate entry, no GPU-rate entries, and no attributed savings from its five cost-reduction mechanisms.

**We can credibly say Akki produces real, governed documents. We cannot yet substantiate “some of the best” or “a fraction of the cost.”** Those claims need matched briefs, blind quality assessment and total cost per accepted output—including retries, review and human correction.

## 4. ARMI — **~35%**

### What is genuinely present
Agents can be represented as distinct principals with mandates and autonomy scopes. Model, skill, admission, task and resource records provide useful foundations. Some model roles already have job descriptions and stated strengths.

### What is missing
One usable operational profile connecting:

**Owner → business objective/JD → abilities and limits → model/skill versions → admission scope → completed work → quality/cost → collaborators → business contribution.**

I checked the actual **Your Agents** screen on desktop and mobile. It explicitly reports that the capability is unavailable.

### An important canon constraint
**The Watch’s private behavioural profiles cannot become ARMI’s employee-style management scores.** Canon expressly protects those profiles from analytics, scoring, pricing and management visibility.

ARMI should use operational task evidence and authorized assurance records—not expose security-profile internals.

Likewise:
- Admission should remain a **scoped pass/hold/reject**, tied to a version and task—not an unexplained flattering percentage.
- “Malicious conduct probability” must not be invented from an LLM judgment or a detector label. It needs an explicitly authorized, calibrated measure; otherwise show evidence-backed incidents, restrictions and uncertainty.
- Revenue contribution needs attribution rules. Completing a task does not prove that an agent caused a revenue increase.

Naming an external agent such as E1 in a registry also does not, by itself, contain its external tools or execution environment.

## 5. Shield and contextual confidentiality — **~40%**

Your **Mode B** clarification is clear: external frontier models may receive safe reconstructed context; privileged originals remain inside.

### What is genuinely present
The active model-call path has de-identification, restoration, purpose and endpoint controls, masking checks and receipts. The recognizer combines patterns, tenant vocabulary and local named-entity recognition.

### The most important missing capability
**Removing names is not the same as protecting sensitive meaning.** A contract can lose every name and still reveal its secret pricing method, negotiating position or proprietary formula.

The current category-preview implementation explicitly matches a category’s **literal name**, not its meaning. A fresh synthetic check matched `unreleased_pricing` but did not recognise a paraphrase describing confidential launch pricing.

Other gaps include:
- The complete DPO-authored declaration lifecycle—generate, review, edit, adopt, approve and governed retirement.
- Semantic category recognition and reliable preservation of useful intent.
- Proof that question, retrieved data, system instructions, memory, tool results and attachments all receive the required treatment.
- Independent question/data assessment and the joint-disclosure check prescribed by canon.
- Representative multilingual privacy-and-utility validation.

I also found that the **wired model-input hostility scorer is a PLACEHOLDER returning “low/pass” from metadata**. This does not mean every Watch mechanism is fictitious; it means that particular connection is not the content-threat detector its surrounding architecture anticipates.

### Correct architectural direction
**Understand and classify privileged material inside the trusted boundary → construct and verify safe context → call the external model → verify and restore only for an entitled recipient.**

We must never send the raw confidential contract to an external model merely to ask what should have been redacted. A stronger external generator does not solve that problem.

**One policy reconciliation remains:** existing canon permits tightly authorized, per-call entity-knowledge disclosure exceptions. Your default promise must explicitly say whether that exceptional mode is disabled or separately disclosed. I have changed neither the policy nor its implementation.

## Recommended delivery approach

Keep one shared operating loop:

**Objective → authorized context → eligible agent/model/skill → governed execution → checks → authorized delivery → cost and outcome record.**

ARMI should expose that loop. Domain products should reuse it. Neither needs another governance layer.
