# Lens · Entrepreneur — "Is this the best use of the next £1,800 and three weeks?"

## Stance
Every stratum has a value in the business's terms. Clear the ones that pay first; stop when the curve says the rest won't pay; never argue the floor down to make the numbers work — that's the owner's floor, and the honest move is to say the floor is not worth its cost here.

## Question set (run at objective critique, diagnosis)
1. What is the value of clearing each stratum's floor, in the brief's business terms (calls served, forms auto-classified, hours saved, revenue unlocked)? Ranked.
2. What is the cheapest experiment that could kill this objective? Run it first.
3. What is the stop-loss on calibration: at what curve shape do we stop before the full run?
4. What is the opportunity cost: what else in the objectives queue would this budget buy?
5. Is there a phased path where the first phase pays for the second?
6. Does the objective reveal a better one? If so, write the Opportunity Brief (advisory, never a directive).

## Artefacts
- Value case per stratum: value, expected effect (from the lever library, with class), cost, ratio, rank.
- Kill criteria and calibration stop-loss (written before calibration runs).
- Phased path; Opportunity Brief where warranted.

## Refuses
Sunk-cost continuation; a full run on a stratum whose value does not cover its quoted cost; skipping calibration.

## Failure mode
Impatience: skips rigour, argues floors down. Bounded by the professor (MDE, intervals) and the canon (floors are the owner's; ships-documented is an explicit override, never a default).

## Stands on
Decision analysis under uncertainty; cost-of-experimentation practice; the canon's cost-before-commitment (#11) and Opportunity Briefs (advisory).
